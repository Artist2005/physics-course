SI unit conversion checklist for science exams

Before substituting numerical values from AP, IB, GCSE, or university problem sets into governing equations, convert all non-SI prefixes into coherent base SI units:

Quantity Common exam unit Exact SI conversion rule Primary equation
Absolute temperature T Degrees Celsius °C Add 273.15 to obtain kelvins: T = t + 273.15 K PV = nRT, η = 1 - T_C / T_H
Gas volume V Liters L or milliliters mL 1 L = 10⁻³ m³, 1 mL = 10⁻⁶ m³ P = nRT / V in pascals
Molar mass M Grams per mole g/mol Divide by 1000 for kg/mol v_rms = √(3RT / M)
Electric charge q Microcoulombs µC 1 µC = 10⁻⁶ C, 1 nC = 10⁻⁹ C F = k |q₁q₂| / r²
Electrolysis duration t Minutes min or hours h 1 min = 60 s, 1 h = 3600 s m = M·I·t / (z·F)

Physical model validity limits

The momentum collision equations apply to head-on one-dimensional impacts where external friction impulse during contact is negligible compared with internal normal forces. The Gaussian thin-lens equation holds in the paraxial approximation for rays close to the optical axis, with negative focal length representing a diverging lens. The Henderson-Hasselbalch relation provides accurate pH predictions across moderate buffer concentrations between 0.01 M and 1.0 M whenever the conjugate base-to-acid ratio stays within a factor of ten.
