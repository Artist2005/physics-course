Three-stage scientific inquiry workflow in action

Every simulation chapter at physics.sheua.com guides learners through three experimental phases. Switch through the steps below using rolling bodies on an inclined plane as an example:

Commit to a testable prediction before running the apparatus

A solid cylinder and a thin hoop of identical mass and radius start rolling without slipping from rest at the top of an incline. Which body reaches the bottom first?

Vary incline angle and compare center-of-mass accelerations

Adjust ramp angle α below to compare linear acceleration for a solid cylinder versus a thin hoop at g = 9.81 m/s²:

α = 30°: a_cylinder = 3.27 m/s², a_hoop = 2.45 m/s²

Analytical derivation from rotational inertia

Gravitational potential energy mgh splits between translational kinetic energy ½mv² and rotational energy ½Iω². A solid cylinder has inertia factor β = I/(mR²) = 0.5 while a thin hoop has β = 1.0:

a = g sin α / (1 + β) ⇒ a_cylinder = (2/3) g sin α, a_hoop = (1/2) g sin α

Neither mass m nor radius R affects the outcome. Explore the full 3D vector apparatus and export a printable lab worksheet in Inclined Plane Dynamics →

Curriculum organization and difficulty tiers

To support differentiated instruction across secondary school curricula such as Grades 8 to 11, GCSE, AP, and IB alongside introductory university courses, every simulation in the dropdown registry belongs to one of two curriculum tiers. The Core Curriculum tier covers foundational laws of mechanics, hydrostatics, ideal gases, geometric optics, atomic structure, acid-base pH, cell organelles, and Mendelian genetics. The Advanced Level tier covers gyroscopic precession, Michelson interferometry, Compton scattering, molecular orbital theory, Born-Haber cycles, Hodgkin-Huxley electrophysiology, and CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing.
