Three-stage scientific inquiry architecture

Each of the 106 chapters guides learners through a structured three-stage experimental workflow designed for independent study and projector-based classroom instruction:

  1. Predictive hypothesis. Before running the apparatus, students analyze a conceptual or quantitative problem and commit to a testable physical hypothesis.
  2. Quantitative 3D experimentation. Learners vary physical parameters across core school and advanced university regimes, inspect real-time 3D force, velocity, field, and dipole vectors, and record live energy oscillograms.
  3. Analytical proof and lab report. The live KaTeX formula calculator substitutes the active slider values directly into the governing equations, solves inverse problems, shares exact parameter URLs for homework, and exports a clean printable laboratory report.

Curriculum organization and difficulty tiers

To support differentiated instruction across secondary school curricula such as Grades 8 to 11, GCSE, AP, and IB alongside introductory university courses, every simulation in the dropdown registry belongs to one of two curriculum tiers. The Core Curriculum tier covers foundational laws of mechanics, hydrostatics, ideal gases, geometric optics, atomic structure, acid-base pH, cell organelles, and Mendelian genetics. The Advanced Level tier covers gyroscopic precession, Michelson interferometry, Compton scattering, molecular orbital theory, Born-Haber cycles, Hodgkin-Huxley electrophysiology, and CRISPR-Cas9 gene editing.
