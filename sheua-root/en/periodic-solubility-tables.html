3. Standard reduction potentials E° in aqueous solution at 298 K

Standard electrode potentials E° are measured at unit solute activity and 1 bar gas pressure relative to the standard hydrogen electrode 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇄ H₂ defined as 0.00 V. Species with strongly negative E° are strong reducing agents and form the anode in galvanic cells, whereas couples with high positive E° act as oxidizing agents at the cathode.

Periodic trends, ionic precipitation rules, and cell voltage predictions

Atomic radius, first ionization energy, and Pauling electronegativity vary systematically across periods and groups because outer valence electrons experience an effective nuclear charge Zeff = Z − σ. Moving left to right across a period adds protons to the nucleus while adding electrons to the same principal shell, pulling orbitals inward, shrinking covalent radius, and raising ionization energy. Moving down a group adds higher principal quantum shells n, increasing atomic radius and metallic reducing character.

In double-replacement aqueous reactions, mixing two soluble electrolytes produces a net ionic reaction whenever an ion pair forms an insoluble precipitate, a volatile gas such as CO₂, SO₂, H₂S, or NH₃, or a weak electrolyte such as water. Quantitatively, precipitation begins as soon as the reaction quotient Qsp exceeds the thermodynamic solubility product Ksp. Similarly, in redox electrochemistry, a spontaneous galvanic reaction satisfies E°_cell = E°_cathode − E°_anode > 0, corresponding to negative standard Gibbs free energy change ΔG° = −zFE°_cell.
