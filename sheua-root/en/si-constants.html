1. Fundamental physical and physicochemical constants in SI

The seven base units of the International System of Units, namely the second s, meter m, kilogram kg, ampere A, kelvin K, mole mol, and candela cd, are defined by fixing the exact numerical values of the vacuum speed of light c, Planck constant h, elementary charge e, Boltzmann constant kB, Avogadro constant NA, cesium hyperfine frequency ΔνCs, and luminous efficacy Kcd. Defining constants carry zero experimental uncertainty.

3. Processing direct and indirect experimental uncertainties

When recording a repeated series of n measurements x₁, x₂, ..., xn in a school or university laboratory, compute the sample arithmetic mean x̄ = (∑ xi) / n and Bessel's unbiased standard error of the mean sx̄ = √(∑(xi − x̄)² / [n(n − 1)]). Multiplying sx̄ by the two-sided Student factor tP, n−1 gives the random confidence half-width Δxrand, which combines in quadrature with the calibrated instrument tolerance Δxinst via Δx = √(Δxrand² + Δxinst²).

For an indirect quantity calculated from a power-law product such as gravitational acceleration g = 4π²L / T² in the Simple Pendulum 3D simulation, independent relative uncertainties add in quadrature weighted by their exponents: εg = √((ΔL / L)² + (2 ΔT / T)²). Because period T enters squared, timing precision contributes twice as strongly to the relative uncertainty of g as ruler length precision, which is why timing 20 or 30 full oscillations reduces ΔT / T dramatically.

Always round the final total uncertainty Δx to one significant digit, or keep two significant digits when the leading digit is 1 or 2, and round the reported mean x̄ to the exact same decimal place.
