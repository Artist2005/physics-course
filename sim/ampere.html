Magnetic Interaction of Parallel Currents & Definition of the Ampere

Two parallel conductors carrying electric currents in the same direction attract each other, and when carrying currents in opposite directions, repel.

Question
Two long parallel conductors separated by distance d carry currents I1 and I2 in the same direction (↑↑). How do the conductors interact, and how does the force per unit length F/l change if both currents are doubled?
Selected answer Option B
By the Biot-Savart Law, current I1 creates a circular magnetic field B1 = (μ0 I1)/(2π d) at the location of the second conductor. Current I2 in this field experiences the Ampère force F12 = I2[l×B1] of magnitude (F)/(l) = (μ0 I1 I2)/(2π d). By the right-hand and left-hand rules, parallel currents (↑↑) attract, whereas antiparallel currents (↑↓) repel. Doubling both I1 and I2 multiplies the product I1 I2, and thus the force, by 4!

Core Curriculum · Grade 9-11
Mechanism

In 1820, André-Marie Ampère discovered the mechanical force between two parallel straight conductors carrying currents I1 and I2 separated by distance d. The interaction is mediated by the magnetic field: wire 1 generates concentric magnetic field lines of magnitude B1 = (μ0 I1)/(2π d) at the location of wire 2 (right-hand grip rule).

Equations and conservation laws

Wire 2 of length L carrying current I2 in field B1 experiences the Ampère force F12 = I2 [L × B1], yielding a mutual force of magnitude **F = (μ0 I1 I2 L)/(2π d)**, where μ0 = 4π× 10-7 H/m is the vacuum permeability.

Measurements and applications

Evaluating the double cross product I2 × (I1 × r12) proves that parallel currents in the same direction attract, whereas antiparallel currents repel! Historically, this exact configuration defined the SI base unit of electric current, the Ampere (2× 10-7 N/m at d = 1 m).

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Left busbar current I₁: 5 … 30 A, standard value 15 A
  • Right busbar current I₂: 5 … 30 A, standard value 15 A
  • Conductor separation d: 1.2 … 3.4 cm, standard value 2 cm
  • Mode (+1 parallel ↑↑, −1 antiparallel ↑↓): -1 … 1, standard value 1
Physical model and application
Two current-carrying wires act like magnets without a single piece of iron. When currents flow in the same direction, the wires pull together; when currents flow in opposite directions, they push apart.
