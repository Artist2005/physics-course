Bernoulli's Equation & Venturi Effect

In steady flow of an ideal incompressible fluid, the sum of static pressure and dynamic pressure head remains constant along any streamline.

Question
Fluid flows horizontally through a Venturi tube from a wide section of area S1 into a narrow throat of area S2 < S1. How do the flow speed v2 and manometer liquid column height h2 above the narrow throat change?
Selected answer Option B
By the continuity equation S1 v1 = S2 v2, fluid speed in the narrow throat increases inversely with cross-sectional area: v2 = v1 (D1/D2)2. By energy conservation (Bernoulli's equation), an increase in dynamic pressure head (1)/(2)ρ v22 is accompanied by a drop in static pressure: P2 = P1 - (1)/(2)ρ(v22 - v12), causing the middle manometer column to drop below the outer columns.
Formula
v2 = v1 ((D1)/(D2)) 2, Δ P = P1 - P2 = (ρ v12)/(2) [((D1)/(D2)) 4 - 1]
v2 = 5.04 m/s, Δ P = P1 - P2 = 11.58 kPa

Core Curriculum · Grade 10
Mechanism

Steady laminar flow of an ideal incompressible fluid (ρ = const) through a variable-cross-section pipe obeys two conservation laws. First is the continuity equation: volume flow rate QV = S1 v1 = S2 v2 = const is invariant along the tube, forcing the fluid to accelerate wherever the cross-section narrows (S2 v1).

Equations and conservation laws

Second is Bernoulli's equation (conservation of mechanical energy per unit volume): along any streamline, the sum of static pressure p, dynamic pressure (1)/(2)ρ v2, and hydrostatic head ρ g h remains constant: p1 + (1)/(2)ρ v12 = p2 + (1)/(2)ρ v22 = const for a horizontal pipe.

Measurements and applications

This explains the Venturi effect: accelerating a fluid parcel into the constriction requires a forward pressure gradient p1 - p2 = (1)/(2)ρ v12 [((S1)/(S2))2 - 1] > 0. Consequently, where flow speed is highest, static pressure against the pipe walls (shown by the manometer column) is lowest!

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Inlet flow speed v₁: 0.6 … 2.8 m/s, standard value 1.5 m/s
  • Wide pipe diameter D₁: 1.8 … 2.6 m, standard value 2.2 m
  • Throat diameter D₂: 0.8 … 1.7 m, standard value 1.2 m
  • Fluid density ρ: 700 … 1400 kg/m³, standard value 1000 kg/m³
Physical model and application
In the narrowed throat of a Venturi tube or nozzle, incompressible fluid flow accelerates by continuity (A1 v1 = A2 v2) while static wall pressure drops according to Bernoulli's principle. The same hydrodynamic mechanism governs atomizers and airfoil lift.
