CRISPR–Cas9 System: Programmable Target DNA Recognition & Precision Genome Editing

"The Cas9-sgRNA ribonucleoprotein acts as a GPS-guided molecular scalpel: upon recognizing a 5′-NGG-3′ PAM sequence, it unwinds target DNA to pair 20 guide nucleotides and executes a precise double-strand break."

Question
The *SpCas9* endonuclease carries a 20-nucleotide single-guide RNA (sgRNA) complementary to a genomic target site. What mandatory short sequence must be present in the DNA immediately adjacent to the target for Cas9 to initiate unwinding, and how does the cell perform a precise sequence replacement when a donor DNA template is supplied?
Selected answer Option B
Programmable genome editing by CRISPR-Cas9 (2020 Nobel Prize, E. Charpentier & J. Doudna) proceeds in three stages: 1) PAM Scanning: Cas9 scans duplex DNA for a **Protospacer Adjacent Motif (5'-NGG-3' for *SpCas9*). Without a valid PAM, Cas9 will not unwind the helix even if the 20-nt target is identical; 2) R-Loop Formation: PAM binding triggers local DNA melting so the 20-nt spacer of the sgRNA base-pairs with the complementary target strand; 3) Double-Strand Break (DSB) & Repair: the HNH domain cleaves the target strand while the RuvC domain cleaves the non-target strand 3 bp upstream of PAM. Cellular machinery then repairs the break via error-prone NHEJ (for gene knockout) or high-fidelity HDR** (using a homologous donor DNA template for precision gene correction).

Mechanism

Derived from bacterial adaptive immunity against bacteriophages, the CRISPR-Cas9 system (Emmanuelle Charpentier & Jennifer Doudna, 2020 Nobel Prize) is a programmable RNA-guided genome editing platform. The active complex pairs the Cas9 endonuclease with a single-guide RNA (sgRNA) carrying a 20-nucleotide target-specific spacer.

Equations and conservation laws

Target interrogation begins when Cas9's PAM-interacting domain recognizes a short **Protospacer Adjacent Motif (5'-NGG-3' for *SpCas9*). PAM binding triggers local DNA duplex melting and directional invasion of the sgRNA spacer to form an R-loop** hybrid with the target DNA strand.

Measurements and applications

Full complementarity across the 20-nt spacer allosterically activates two catalytic nuclease domains: HNH cleaves the target strand paired to sgRNA, while RuvC cleaves the displaced non-target strand 3 bp upstream of PAM, creating a blunt double-strand break (DSB). Host repair proceeds via NHEJ (generating indel knockouts) or Homology-Directed Repair (HDR) using an exogenous donor template for precise sequence replacement.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Guide RNA-DNA mismatches Nmm (0 = perfect 20/20 complementarity): 0 … 6 mismatches, standard value 0 mismatches
  • Presence of 5′-NGG-3′ PAM motif adjacent to target (1: intact, 0: mutated): 0 … 1 PAM, standard value 1 PAM
  • Homologous donor DNA template availability for HDR repair: 0 … 100 %, standard value 65 %
  • Cas9-sgRNA ribonucleoprotein concentration: 10 … 100 nM, standard value 50 nM
Physical model and application
The CRISPR-Cas9 genome-editing complex functions as a programmable site-specific endonuclease: single-guide RNA (sgRNA) carries a 20-nucleotide spacer complementary to the target locus, and once Cas9 verifies the adjacent 5'-NGG-3' PAM motif, it unwinds the duplex and cleaves both DNA strands for subsequent repair.
