Biophysics of Vision: Optical Power of the Eye, Lens Accommodation & Spectacle Correction

"The optical system of the human eye projects a real, inverted, and reduced image onto the retina; sharp focusing across distances is achieved by reflex adjustment of the elastic crystalline lens curvature."

Question
In nearsightedness (myopia), the eyeball's axial length is elongated (e.g., L = 25.5 mm instead of the normal 24.0 mm) or refractive power is excessive. Where do parallel light rays from a distant object come to a focus in an uncorrected myopic eye, and what type of corrective spectacle lens restores sharp focus on the retina?
Selected answer Option B
The eye's refractive apparatus combines the cornea (Dcornea ≈ 43 dpt) and the adjustable crystalline lens (Dlens ≈ 15..29 dpt), obeying the thin-lens relation D = (1)/(d) + (1)/(f) to focus rays onto retinal photoreceptors (f = Leye). In myopia (nearsightedness), the eyeball is too long (Leye > f), so parallel rays from distant objects converge in front of the retina, creating a blur circle on the macula. A **diverging lens (Dglasses 0)** is prescribed.

Core Curriculum · Grade 8-11 · Biophysics of Vision
Mechanism

The optical system of the human eye combines fixed refraction at the anterior cornea (Dcornea ≈ 43 dpt) with variable refraction by the elastic crystalline lens (Dlens ≈ 15..29 dpt), projecting a real, inverted, and diminished image onto the retinal photoreceptors (rods and cones).

Equations and conservation laws

Accommodation is the reflex adjustment of lens curvature: for distant vision, the ciliary muscle relaxes and zonular fibers pull the lens flat. For near vision, ciliary muscle contraction slackens the zonules, allowing the elastic lens capsule to round up and increase optical power according to D = (1)/(d) + (1)/(f).

Measurements and applications

In emmetropia, the axial length (L ≈ 24.0 mm) matches the focal length. In myopia (nearsightedness), an elongated globe (L > f) places the focus in front of the retina, corrected with a **diverging lens (D 0)**.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Viewing object distance d (25 cm reading, 500 cm far vision): 25 … 500 cm, standard value 200 cm
  • Eyeball axial length L (24.0 mm emmetropic, >24 myopia, <24 hyperopia): 22 … 26 mm, standard value 24 mm
  • Corrective spectacle lens power Dglasses (− diverging, + converging): -6 … 6 dpt, standard value 0 dpt
  • Ciliary muscle accommodation power ΔDaccom: 0 … 6 dpt, standard value 0.5 dpt
Physical model and application
The optical system of the human eye focuses images onto the retina via accommodation, as the ciliary muscle adjusts the curvature and refractive power of the crystalline lens. In a myopic (elongated) eye, parallel rays from distant objects converge in front of the retina, requiring a diverging corrective lens of negative optical power (D < 0) to shift the focal plane onto the retina.
