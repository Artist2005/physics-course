Oxygen Transport by Hemoglobin: Allosteric Cooperativity (T ↔ R) & the Bohr Effect

"Binding of the first O₂ molecule to a ferrous Fe²⁺ heme triggers a quaternary T → R transition across the α₂β₂ hemoglobin tetramer, increasing the oxygen affinity of the remaining subunits."

Question
The oxyhemoglobin dissociation curve Y = ((pO2)n)/((P50)n + (pO2)n) is sigmoidal with Hill coefficient n ≈ 2.8, whereas monomeric myoglobin exhibits a hyperbolic curve (n = 1). How and why do half-saturation pressure P50 and tissue O2 delivery change in exercising muscle when pH drops and pCO2 rises (the Bohr effect)?
Selected answer Option B
Adult hemoglobin A is an α2β2 tetramer containing four prosthetic heme groups, each with a ferrous Fe2+ ion (which remains Fe2+ during reversible oxygenation). Positive cooperativity (n ≈ 2.8) ensures nearly full saturation (≈ 98%) in alveolar capillaries (pO2 ≈ 100 mmHg) and steep unloading in peripheral tissues (pO2 ≈ 25-40 mmHg). By Christian Bohr's effect, exercising muscle produces CO2, lactic acid (H+, lowering pH from 7.4 to 7.2), and heat; protonation of key histidine residues stabilizes the low-affinity T-state, raising P50 (rightward shift) and boosting O2 delivery to working cells.

Mechanism

Adult **hemoglobin A (extHbA)** is an allosteric α2β2 tetramer carrying four prosthetic heme groups, each containing a ferrous **Fe2+** center (whose +2 oxidation state remains unchanged during reversible oxygenation). Unlike monomeric muscle myoglobin (n = 1, hyperbolic curve with P50 ≈ 2.8 mmHg), hemoglobin exhibits positive cooperativity.

Equations and conservation laws

Binding of the first O2 ligand pulls Fe2+ into the porphyrin plane and triggers a quaternary transition from the tense, low-affinity T-state (deoxyhemoglobin) to the relaxed, high-affinity R-state (oxyhemoglobin). Described by the Hill equation Y = ((pO2)n)/((P50)n + (pO2)n) (n ≈ 2.8, P50 ≈ 26.8 mmHg), this sigmoidal curve guarantees ≈ 98% loading in the lungs (100 mmHg) and steep unloading in systemic capillaries (25-40 mmHg).

Measurements and applications

By the Bohr effect, metabolically active tissues release CO2, H+ (lowering blood pH), and heat; together with erythrocyte 2,3-BPG, these allosteric effectors stabilize the T-state, increase P50, and shift the dissociation curve to the right and increases O2 unloading in working tissues.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Oxygen partial pressure pO₂ (40=venous tissue, 100=alveolar lung): 10 … 110 mmHg, standard value 40 mmHg
  • Blood pH (Bohr effect: 7.40=normal, 7.15=exercising acidosis): 7 … 7.6 pH units, standard value 7.4 pH units
  • Blood temperature t (hyperthermia shifts curve right): 35 … 41 °C, standard value 37 °C
  • Erythrocyte 2,3-BPG level (high-altitude adaptation): 0.5 … 2 rel., standard value 1 rel.
Physical model and application
Tetrameric hemoglobin (4 subunits bearing Fe2+ hemes) binds oxygen cooperatively: oxygenation of the first heme in pulmonary capillaries triggers a conformational shift from the low-affinity T-state to the high-affinity R-state, accelerating binding at the remaining sites. In metabolically active tissues rich in H+ and CO2, the Bohr effect stabilizes the T-state and promotes O2 unloading.
