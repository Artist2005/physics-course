Meiotic Cell Division: Bivalent Synapsis, Crossing-Over & Combinatorial Diversity

"During Prophase I of meiosis, homologous chromosomes pair into bivalents (tetrads) and exchange allelic segments at chiasmata, transforming one diploid cell (2n4c) into four genetically distinct haploid gametes (1n1c)."

Question
During Prophase I of meiosis, homologous chromosomes undergo synapsis and crossing-over between linked genes A and B located d = 18 cM (centimorgans) apart on the same chromosome. What is the combined percentage of recombinant gametes (Ab and aB), and what is the chromosome-chromatid formula of each of the four resulting cells at the end of Meiosis II?
Selected answer Option B
Meiosis comprises two successive nuclear divisions following a single round of DNA replication (2n4c). In Prophase I, homologous chromosomes pair (synapse) into bivalents (tetrads of four chromatids) and exchange non-sister chromatid segments at chiasmata (crossing-over). By Morgan's linear linkage principle, map distance d in centimorgans equals the percentage of recombinant gametes: RF = (Nrec)/(Ntotal) · 100% = 18% (9% Ab and 9% aB, while parental AB and ab gametes make up 82%). In Anaphase I, homologous chromosomes segregate to opposite poles (reductional division: 2n4c → 1n2c), and in Anaphase II sister chromatids separate (equational division: 1n2c → 1n1c), yielding four unique haploid gametes.

Core Curriculum · Grade 9-11 · Meiosis & Crossing-Over
Mechanism

Meiosis is a specialized two-stage nuclear division that reduces chromosome ploidy from diploid (**2n4c after interphase S-phase) to four genetically distinct haploid cells (1n1c gametes or spores). During Prophase I, maternal and paternal homologous chromosomes align lengthwise via the synaptonemal complex (synapsis), forming bivalents (tetrads of four chromatids)**.

Equations and conservation laws

At physical crossover junctions (chiasmata), non-sister chromatids break and rejoin in homologous recombination (crossing-over). By Thomas Hunt Morgan's genetic mapping law, the recombination frequency RF between two linked loci defines map distance: **1 centimorgan (cM) = 1% recombinant gametes** (RF ≤ 50%).

Measurements and applications

In Anaphase I (reductional division), homologous chromosomes segregate to opposite poles (**2n4c → 1n2c**), generating 2n combinations by independent assortment. In Meiosis II (equational division), sister chromatids separate at Anaphase II to yield **four haploid 1n1c cells**.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Meiosis stage (1: Prophase I chiasma, 2: Anaphase I, 3: Metaphase II, 4: Gametes 1n1c): 1 … 4 stage, standard value 1 stage
  • Genetic map distance d between linked loci A and B: 2 … 48 cM, standard value 18 cM
  • Number of chiasmata (crossover points) per bivalent: 1 … 3 chiasmata, standard value 2 chiasmata
  • Haploid chromosome number (bivalent pairs n): 2 … 4 pairs, standard value 3 pairs
Physical model and application
Genetic recombination during meiosis generates allelic diversity among gametes: in Prophase I, homologous paternal and maternal chromosomes pair into bivalents (synapsis) and exchange corresponding chromatid segments at chiasmata (crossing-over), producing four haploid cells (1n1c) with recombinant gene combinations after two meiotic divisions.
