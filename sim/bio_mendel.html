Mendelian Genetics & Hardy–Weinberg Equilibrium: Genotype Frequency Dynamics

"In a large random-mating population free from selection, mutation, and migration, allele frequencies p and q remain constant across generations, and genotype frequencies AA, Aa, and aa follow the binomial expansion (p + q)² = p² + 2pq + q² = 1."

Question
In a population at Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, the frequency of the recessive phenotype (homozygous aa) is q2 = 0.16 (16%). What are the frequencies of the recessive allele q, the dominant allele p = 1 - q, and the heterozygous carrier genotype 2pq (Aa)?
Selected answer Option B
Under Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, alternative allele frequencies sum to unity (p + q = 1) and genotype frequencies are f(AA) = p2, f(Aa) = 2pq, and f(aa) = q2. Since the recessive phenotype occurs only in aa homozygotes, q2 = 0.16 yields q = √(0.16) = 0.40. The dominant allele frequency is p = 1 - q = 0.60. Homozygous dominants AA represent p2 = 0.36 (36%), while heterozygous carriers Aa represent 2pq = 2 · 0.60 · 0.40 = 0.48 (48%). When p = q = 0.5 (monohybrid cross Aa × Aa), we recover Mendel's classic 1:2:1 genotype ratio (25% : 50% : 25%) and 3:1 dominant-to-recessive phenotype ratio.

Core Curriculum · Grade 9-11 · Mendelian & Population Genetics
Mechanism

Gregor Mendel's laws of segregation (1:2:1 genotypic and 3:1 phenotypic ratios in a monohybrid cross Aa × Aa) and independent assortment (9:3:3:1) arise from meiotic allele separation and random gamete union visualized by the Punnett square.

Equations and conservation laws

At the population level, the Hardy-Weinberg principle (1908) states that in a large, random-mating diploid population free from selection, mutation, migration, and drift, allele frequencies p = f(A) and q = f(a) (p + q = 1) remain constant across generations.

Measurements and applications

Genotype frequencies obey the binomial expansion (p + q)2 = p2 + 2pq + q2 = 1, where **p2** is the homozygous dominant frequency (AA), **2pq** is the heterozygous carrier frequency (Aa, peaking at 0.50 when p = q = 0.5), and **q2** is the homozygous recessive frequency (aa). Under selection s against aa, mean fitness is w = 1 - s q2 and the next-generation allele frequency shifts to p' = (p2 + pq)/(1 - s q2).

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Dominant allele frequency p (q = 1 − p; p = 0.5 → Mendel 3:1): 0.1 … 0.9, standard value 0.6
  • Inheritance mode (1=Complete dominance 3:1, 0.5=Incomplete dominance 1:2:1): 0.5 … 1, standard value 1
  • Natural selection coefficient s against recessive homozygotes aa: 0 … 0.8, standard value 0
  • Sampled offspring population size Nind: 50 … 500 ind., standard value 200 ind.
Physical model and application
If a bag holds 60% blue marbles (p=0.6) and 40% yellow marbles (q=0.4), drawing two at random gives two blue with probability p2 = 0.36, two yellow with q2 = 0.16, and a mixed pair in two ways with 2pq = 0.48. That binomial law is Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium.
