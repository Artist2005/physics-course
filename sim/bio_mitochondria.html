Mitchell’s Chemiosmotic Theory: Proton-Motive Force Δp & Rotary ATP Synthesis

"Energy released by NADH oxidation along the inner mitochondrial membrane is first transduced into an electrochemical proton gradient ΔμH+, which drives the rotary molecular turbine of F₀F₁-ATP synthase."

Question
According to Peter Mitchell's chemiosmotic theory, the proton-motive force across the inner mitochondrial membrane is Δ p = Δψ - (2.303 RT)/(F)ΔpH (≈ 61.5 mV per pH unit at 37 °C). How do ATP synthesis and heat dissipation change when a protonophore or uncoupling protein (thermogenin UCP1 in brown adipose tissue) increases proton leak bypassing F0F1-ATP synthase?
Selected answer Option B
Respiratory Complexes I, III, and IV pump H+ from the matrix into the intermembrane space, building a proton-motive force Δ p = Δψ + 61.5 (pHmatrix - pHIMS) ≈ 180-220 mV. Normally, H+ reentry through the F0 c-ring rotates the central γ-stalk inside the F1 head, synthesizing 3 ATP per revolution. Protonophores or UCP1 create a proton shunt across the membrane: Δ p diminishes, ATP phosphorylation falls, and respiratory redox energy is quantitatively converted into thermal energy Q.

Mechanism

According to Peter Mitchell's chemiosmotic theory (Nobel Prize, 1978), electron transfer from NADH and FADH2 to O2 along the inner mitochondrial membrane (cristae) is coupled to outward proton translocation across Complexes I, III, and IV from the matrix into the intermembrane space (IMS).

Equations and conservation laws

This establishes an electrochemical proton gradient quantified by the proton-motive force Δ p = Δψ - (2.303 RT)/(F) ΔpH ≈ Δψ + 61.5 (pHmatrix - pHIMS) mV, combining the membrane electrical potential (Δψ ≈ 150-180 mV, matrix negative) and the transmembrane pH difference.

Measurements and applications

Downhill proton return through the F0 c-ring drives mechanical rotation of the central γ-stalk inside the F1 catalytic hexamer (Paul Boyer's binding-change mechanism), synthesizing 3 ATP molecules per 360° revolution. Uncoupling proteins (UCP1 / thermogenin) create a proton leak bypassing F0F1, dissipating Δ p directly as heat.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Electron donor flux (NADH / FADH₂) into respiratory chain: 0.4 … 2.5 rel., standard value 1.2 rel.
  • Inner membrane electrical potential Δψ: 80 … 190 mV, standard value 150 mV
  • Transmembrane pH gradient ΔpH (pHmatrix − pHIMS): 0.2 … 1.5 pH units, standard value 0.8 pH units
  • Proton leak / uncoupler UCP1 (0=coupled, 1=shunted): 0 … 0.9, standard value 0.05
Physical model and application
The inner mitochondrial membrane works like a hydroelectric dam. Respiratory complexes pump H+ protons into an upper reservoir, and F0F1-ATP synthase is a rotary nano-turbine spinning as protons rush back to mint ATP. Opening a bypass spillway (uncoupling protein UCP1 in brown fat) lets protons leak without spinning the turbine, generating pure body heat.
