Mitotic Cell Division: Spindle Microtubule Mechanics & Ploidy Conservation

"Equal partitioning of the replicated genome into daughter cells is orchestrated by the bipolar mitotic spindle: kinetochore microtubules align chromosomes at the equator and synchronously pull sister chromatids to opposite poles."

Question
A diploid somatic cell (2n = 4 in a model organism) completes DNA replication during S-phase and enters mitosis. What are the chromosome number (n) and DNA content (c) at the metaphase plate, and at each spindle pole immediately after centromere splitting in anaphase?
Selected answer Option B
DNA replication occurs during interphase S-phase, so the cell enters prophase and metaphase with 2n4c: there are 2n chromosomes, each composed of two identical sister chromatids (4c DNA) held together by cohesin at the centromere. At anaphase onset, separase cleaves cohesin and each separated chromatid becomes an independent daughter chromosome. Temporarily the dividing cell contains 4n4c, meaning each spindle pole receives a genetically identical diploid complement of 2n2c.

Mechanism

Mitosis is the division of a eukaryotic somatic cell that partitions a replicated genome equally into two genetically identical daughter cells (2n → 2n). Because DNA replication completes during interphase S-phase (2n2c → 2n4c), chromosomes enter mitosis composed of two sister chromatids joined at the centromere by cohesin.

Equations and conservation laws

During prophase, chromatin condenses and the bipolar mitotic spindle assembles from dynamic tubulin microtubules. In metaphase, amphitelic attachment of kinetochore microtubules balances opposing pulling forces (Fkin ≈ 10-50 pN), aligning all 2n4c chromosomes on the equatorial metaphase plate.

Measurements and applications

Once the spindle assembly checkpoint (SAC) is satisfied, separase cleaves cohesin to initiate anaphase: sister chromatids separate as independent chromosomes (transiently 4n4c in the cell) and move poleward via microtubule depolymerization (Anaphase A) and spindle elongation (Anaphase B), followed by nuclear envelope reformation in telophase and cytokinesis (2 × 2n2c).

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Mitosis stage (1=Prophase, 2=Metaphase, 3=Anaphase, 4=Telophase): 1 … 4, standard value 2
  • Number of homologous chromosome pairs n (diploid 2n): 1 … 3 pairs, standard value 2 pairs
  • Kinetochore microtubule pulling tension Fkin: 0.5 … 2.5 rel., standard value 1.4 rel.
  • Spindle elongation speed (Anaphase B pole separation): 0.5 … 2 rel., standard value 1 rel.
Physical model and application
Before mitosis, the cell photocopies every chromosome book during S-phase and staples the twin copies together into an X-shape (2n4c). In metaphase they line up along the equator, and in anaphase the staple is cut so spindle fibers pull one complete set (2n2c) to each daughter pole.
