Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR): Thermal Cycling & Exponential DNA Amplification

"Invented by Kary Mullis, the Polymerase Chain Reaction amplifies a single target DNA molecule into billions of exact copies within hours using thermostable Taq polymerase and programmable temperature cycles."

Question
Each PCR cycle steps through three temperatures: denaturation (95 °C), primer annealing (55..62 °C), and extension (72 °C). Why is DNA polymerase from the thermophilic bacterium *Thermus aquaticus* (*Taq* polymerase) essential for PCR, and how many target DNA copies are generated from N0 = 1 initial template after n = 20 ideal cycles (E = 1.0)?
Selected answer Option B
Conventional mesophilic DNA polymerases irreversibly denature above 65..70 °C. Taq polymerase, isolated from the hot-spring extremophile *Thermus aquaticus*, withstands repeated heating to **95 °C (where hydrogen bonds melt to separate double-stranded DNA) and exhibits optimal catalytic activity at 72 °C. During annealing (54..62 °C)**, forward and reverse oligonucleotide primers hybridize to flanking 3' template regions. Because every newly synthesized strand acts as a template in the next cycle, copy number amplifies exponentially as **N(n) = N0 · (1 + E)n** (for E = 1, N = N0 · 2n, producing over a million copies in 20 cycles and over a billion in 30 cycles).

Mechanism

The Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR, Kary Mullis, 1983) exponentially amplifies a defined DNA segment *in vitro* through automated thermal cycling. Each cycle comprises three steps: **1) Denaturation (94..96 °C) to melt duplex DNA into single strands; 2) Annealing (54..62 °C) to hybridize forward and reverse oligonucleotide primers** to 3' flanking sequences; and **3) Extension (72 °C)** to synthesize complementary 5'→ 3' strands.

Equations and conservation laws

PCR relies on thermostable Taq DNA polymerase from the thermophilic bacterium *Thermus aquaticus*, which survives repeated 95 °C denaturation steps and catalyzes rapid dNTP incorporation at 72 °C in the presence of Mg2+ ions.

Measurements and applications

Because newly synthesized strands become templates in subsequent cycles, target copy number grows exponentially as **N(n) = N0 · (1 + E)n** (yielding N = N0 · 2n for ideal efficiency E = 1, or 220 ≈ 1.05× 106-fold amplification in 20 cycles). In real-time quantitative PCR (qPCR), the threshold cycle **Ct** scales inversely with lg N0.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Completed PCR amplification cycles n: 1 … 30 cycles, standard value 20 cycles
  • Thermal cycle stage (1: Denaturation 95 °C, 2: Annealing 58 °C, 3: Extension 72 °C): 1 … 3 stage, standard value 3 stage
  • Initial template DNA molecules N₀: 1 … 100 templates, standard value 10 templates
  • Per-cycle amplification efficiency E (1.00 = ideal doubling 2ⁿ): 0.7 … 1, standard value 0.98
Physical model and application
The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) exponentially amplifies a specific target DNA sequence *in vitro*. During each three-step thermal cycle (denaturation at 95 °C, primer annealing at 58 °C, extension at 72 °C), thermostable *Taq* polymerase doubles the number of target templates (N = N0 · 2n), generating 220 = 1,048,576 copies from a single molecule in 20 cycles.
