Chloroplast Photosynthesis: Z-Scheme Electron Transport & Calvin Cycle CO₂ Fixation

"Thylakoid chlorophylls transduce photon energy hν into NADPH and ATP while splitting water to release O₂, enabling RuBisCO in the stroma to fix atmospheric CO₂ into hexose sugars."

Question
During non-cyclic photophosphorylation in the chloroplast (the Z-scheme) followed by the Calvin cycle, one molecule of glucose C6H12O6 is synthesized. Which substrate donates the electrons and all evolved molecular oxygen O2, and how many ATP and NADPH molecules are consumed by the Calvin cycle to fix 6 CO2?
Selected answer Option B
Isotopic tracer experiments with H218O proved that all photosynthetic O2 comes from water splitting at the Mn4CaO5 oxygen-evolving complex of Photosystem II (P680): 2H2O →(4hν) O2↑ + 4H+ + 4e-. Electrons travel via plastoquinone, cytochrome b6f, and plastocyanin to Photosystem I (P700) to reduce NADP+ to NADPH, while the lumen-to-stroma proton gradient drives CF0CF1-ATP synthase. In the stroma, fixing each CO2 molecule in the Calvin-Benson cycle requires 3 ATP and 2 NADPH, totaling 18 ATP and 12 NADPH per molecule of glucose (extC6H12O6).

Core Curriculum · Grade 9-11 · Photosynthesis & Calvin Cycle
Mechanism

Photosynthesis in chloroplasts comprises the light-dependent reactions on thylakoid membranes and the Calvin-Benson carbon-fixation cycle in the stroma. Chlorophylls a and b absorb strongly in the blue (430-450 nm) and red (660-680 nm) spectral bands while reflecting green light (∼ 540 nm).

Equations and conservation laws

Along the non-cyclic Z-scheme, photon absorption excites Photosystem II (P680), which extracts electrons from water at the Mn4CaO5 oxygen-evolving complex (water photolysis: 2H2O →(4hν) O2↑ + 4H+ + 4e-, the sole source of evolved O2). Electrons pass through plastoquinone and cytochrome b6f (building a lumenal H+ gradient for CF0CF1-ATP synthase) to Photosystem I (P700), which reduces NADP+ to NADPH.

Measurements and applications

In the stroma, RuBisCO catalyzes CO2 carboxylation of ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate. Synthesizing 1 molecule of glucose (C6H12O6) from 6 CO2 in the Calvin cycle consumes **18 ATP and 12 NADPH**.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Photosynthetically active irradiance PAR I: 50 … 1800 μmol/(m²·s), standard value 800 μmol/(m²·s)
  • Ambient carbon dioxide concentration [CO₂]: 100 … 1000 ppm, standard value 420 ppm
  • Illumination wavelength λ (chlorophyll peaks at 440 nm & 670 nm): 420 … 700 nm, standard value 660 nm
  • Leaf temperature t (RuBisCO optimum ~25 °C): 10 … 40 °C, standard value 25 °C
Physical model and application
Photosynthesis in the chloroplast proceeds through two coupled stages: light-dependent reactions in the thylakoid membranes capture photons, split water (releasing molecular O2), and generate ATP and NADPH; subsequently, the Calvin cycle in the stroma consumes 18 ATP and 12 NADPH to fix 6 CO2 molecules into one molecule of glucose.
