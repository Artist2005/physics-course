Gene Expression & Translation: Ribosomal mRNA Decoding & Polypeptide Elongation

"Genetic information is read from mRNA in the 5′→3′ direction in non-overlapping nucleotide triplets (codons): within the ribosomal A, P, and E sites, aminoacyl-tRNA anticodons translate nucleotide sequences into polypeptides."

Question
Each amino acid in a polypeptide chain is encoded by one mRNA triplet (codon) of 3 nucleotides, while the termination stop codon (UAA, UAG, or UGA) does not encode an amino acid. How many nucleotides are in the coding region of mature mRNA (including the AUG start codon and one stop codon), and how many high-energy ATP/GTP equivalents are consumed to synthesize a protein of Naa = 100 amino acids (4 equivalents per residue)?
Selected answer Option B
The genetic code is triplet and non-overlapping: every 3 consecutive mRNA nucleotides specify one amino acid (starting with AUG for methionine). Translation termination is triggered by a stop codon (UAA, UAG, UGA) recognized by release factors (RF) rather than a tRNA. Thus, a polypeptide of Naa residues requires Nnt = 3 Naa + 3 coding nucleotides (303 nt for 100 aa). Incorporating each residue consumes 2 high-energy bonds during aminoacyl-tRNA charging (ATP → AMP + PPi) plus 2 GTP molecules on the ribosome (EF-Tu and EF-G), totaling 4 Naa = 400 ATP/GTP equivalents.

Core Curriculum · Grade 10-11 · Genetic Code & Translation
Mechanism

In the Central Dogma of Molecular Biology, genetic information flows from DNA via transcription into messenger RNA (mRNA) and via ribosomal translation into protein. The genetic code is triplet, non-overlapping, and degenerate: out of 43 = 64 codons, 61 encode amino acids (starting at AUG for methionine) while 3 (extUAA, UAG, UGA) are termination stop codons.

Equations and conservation laws

The ribosome (80S = 40S + 60S in eukaryotes; 70S = 30S + 50S in prokaryotes, stabilized by Mg2+) houses three tRNA-binding pockets: the A-site (aminoacyl entry delivered by EF-Tu·GTP), the P-site (peptidyl-tRNA holding the nascent chain), and the E-site (deacylated tRNA exit).

Measurements and applications

The rRNA ribozyme of the large subunit's peptidyl transferase center catalyzes peptide bond formation (-CO-NH-), transferring the polypeptide onto the A-site residue. Elongation factor EF-G then hydrolyzes GTP to translocate the ribosome by exactly one codon (3 nt) along mRNA in the 5' → 3' direction, consuming ≈ 4 high-energy phosphate equivalents per amino acid.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Ribosome translocation speed vrib (codons/s): 0.4 … 2.5 rel., standard value 1.2 rel.
  • Target polypeptide chain length Naa: 20 … 200 aa, standard value 100 aa
  • Mg²⁺ concentration (40S+60S / 30S+50S subunit stabilization): 0.5 … 5 mM, standard value 2.5 mM
  • Codon state in A-site (1=Wild-type UUC-Phe, 2=Silent UUU, 3=Missense UAC-Tyr): 1 … 3, standard value 1
Physical model and application
The ribosome is a molecular 3D printer reading an mRNA tape three letters at a time (a triplet codon). tRNA couriers match their anticodon to each triplet and add an amino acid until the ribosome hits a STOP codon (UAA, UAG, UGA), which adds no amino acid and releases the finished protein.
