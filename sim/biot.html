Biot–Savart Law & Uniform Field of Helmholtz Coils

Two coaxial coils separated by a distance equal to their radius transform the curved field of a single loop into a flat uniform plateau.

Question
At what separation d between two identical coaxial circular coils of radius R carrying co-directional currents does the magnetic field near the midpoint become maximally uniform (Helmholtz condition)?
Selected answer Option B
For a single circular loop B(x) = (μ0 NIR2)/(2(R2+x2)3/2), the inflection points where (d2B)/(dx2) = 0 lie at x = ± R/2. Placing two identical coils at separation d = R positions both inflection points at the midpoint, cancelling both first and second derivatives of B(x).
Formula
B(x) = (μ0 N I R2)/(2)[(1)/((R2 + (x - d/2)2)3/2) + (1)/((R2 + (x + d/2)2)3/2)]

Mechanism

By the Biot-Savart Law, a current element I dl produces a magnetic field dB = (μ0 I)/(4π)([dl × r])/(r3). Integrating around a circular coil of radius R and N turns at axial distance x cancels all radial components and yields the axial field **B1(x) = (μ0 N I R2)/(2(R2 + x2)3/2)**.

Equations and conservation laws

A single loop's field peaks at x = 0 but curves downward rapidly ((d2 B1)/(dx2) < 0). To create a wide region of uniform magnetic field with open optical and mechanical access, Hermann von Helmholtz paired two identical coaxial coils of radius R carrying parallel currents separated by distance d: B(x) = B1(x + d/2) + B1(x - d/2).

Measurements and applications

At the midpoint x = 0, symmetry guarantees (dB)/(dx)|0 = 0 for any separation d. Setting the separation equal to the coil radius (**Helmholtz condition d = R**) places the inflection points of the two coils directly at x = 0, making both (d2 B)/(dx2)|0 = 0 and (d3 B)/(dx3)|0 = 0 vanish simultaneously! This creates a flat plateau of uniform field **B(0) = ((4)/(5)) 3/2(μ0 N I)/(R) ≈ 0.7155(μ0 N I)/(R)**.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Coil winding current I: 1 … 10 A, standard value 5 A
  • Coil radius R: 1.2 … 2.4 dm, standard value 1.8 dm
  • Separation-to-radius ratio d/R (1.0 = Helmholtz): 0.5 … 2, standard value 1
  • Turns per coil N: 10 … 40, standard value 20
Physical model and application
A single current loop creates a magnetic field that drops off quickly away from its center. Place two identical coils apart by a distance equal to their radius (d = R), and their fields blend into a remarkably flat plateau of uniform magnetic field right between them.
