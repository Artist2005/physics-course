Light Wavefront Polarization & Malus's Law

Because light is a transverse wave, a crystalline analyzer transmits only the projection of E parallel to its optical axis.

Question
A linearly polarized light beam of intensity I0 strikes a second polarizer (analyzer) whose transmission axis is rotated by θ = 60° relative to the beam's polarization plane. What intensity I emerges from the analyzer?
Selected answer Option B
The analyzer passes only the electric field component E = E0 cos θ along its axis. Since optical intensity is proportional to the square of field amplitude (I ∝ E2), we obtain Malus's Law: I(θ) = I0 cos 2θ. At θ = 60°, cos 60° = 0.5 so I = 0.25 I0, and at θ = 90° (crossed polarizers) the wavefront is completely blocked.

Mechanism

Because light is a transverse electromagnetic wave (E ⊥ k), the electric field vector E oscillates in the plane perpendicular to propagation. Unpolarized natural light consists of rapidly varying random polarization orientations, whereas a linear polarizer transmits only the component of E parallel to its transmission axis.

Equations and conservation laws

When linearly polarized light of amplitude E0 and intensity I0 ∝ E02 encounters a second polarizer (analyzer) rotated by angle θ, only the projected amplitude E = E0 cos θ passes through. Since optical intensity scales as the square of the electric field amplitude, the transmitted intensity obeys Malus's Law: I(θ) = I0 cos 2θ, vanishing completely when the axes are crossed (θ = 90°).

Measurements and applications

Polarization also occurs by reflection off a dielectric of refractive index n. At Brewster's angle tan θB = n (where reflected and refracted rays are orthogonal, θB + γ = 90°), the p-polarized component cannot radiate along the reflection direction, rendering the reflected beam 100% linearly s-polarized, which is the principle behind anti-glare polarized sunglasses.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Analyzer axis angle θ: 0 … 90 °, standard value 35 °
  • Polarized intensity I₀: 40 … 160 W/m², standard value 100 W/m²
  • Light wavelength λ: 420 … 680 nm, standard value 530 nm
  • Glass refractive index n (for θB): 1.3 … 1.9, standard value 1.52
Physical model and application
Light reflected from a horizontal dielectric surface (such as water or glass) at Brewster's angle becomes completely linearly polarized parallel to the interface. A polarizing analyzer oriented with a vertical transmission axis extinguishes this glare in accordance with Malus's law I = I0 cos 2θ.
