Surface Tension, Meniscus Curvature & Capillary Action

A narrow capillary tube makes visible the molecular cohesive forces capable of lifting a liquid column against gravity.

Prediction Problem
What happens to the liquid level inside a narrow glass capillary if the liquid does not wet the walls (contact angle \theta > 90^\circ, e.g., mercury on glass)?
Your hypothesis: Option B
By Jurin's formula h = \frac{2\sigma\cos\theta}{\rho g r}, the sign of h is governed by \cos\theta. For wetting liquids (\theta 0), the concave meniscus pulls the column upward (h > 0), whereas for non-wetting liquids (\theta > 90^\circ, \cos\theta < 0), the convex meniscus depresses the column downward (h < 0).
