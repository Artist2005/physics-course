Carbon Nanostructures & Allotropes: Buckminsterfullerene C₆₀, Nanotubes & Graphene

Carbon is nature's supreme architect: from the same atom it builds the hardest diamond, one-atom-thick graphene, and the soccer-ball cage of fullerene C₆₀.

Question
Why does graphene conduct electricity like a semimetal, and how many pentagonal and hexagonal faces form the truncated icosahedral cage of fullerene C60?
Selected answer Option B
In graphene, carbon nanotubes (CNTs), and fullerenes, carbon atoms are predominantly sp2-hybridized: three σ-bonds form a honeycomb lattice while the fourth 2pz electron enters a delocalized π-system. By Euler's polyhedron theorem (V - E + F = 2), closing a hexagonal sheet into a sphere requires **exactly 12 pentagons**; in C60 (V=60, E=90, F=32) there are 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons. For (n,0) zigzag nanotubes, the tube is metallic when n is a multiple of 3.

Mechanism

Carbon nanostructures, 2D graphene, 1D carbon nanotubes (CNTs), and 0D fullerenes (C60), share an sp2-hybridized carbon framework in which three σ-bonds form a honeycomb lattice while unhybridized 2pz orbitals merge into a delocalized π-conduction network.

Equations and conservation laws

In graphene, the π valence band and π^* conduction band meet at massless Dirac cones (Eg = 0). Rolling graphene along chiral vector (n,m) produces a single-walled carbon nanotube of diameter d = (a√(3))/(π)√(n2+nm+m2), which is metallic when (n-m) is divisible by 3 and semiconducting otherwise.

Measurements and applications

By Euler's polyhedron theorem (V - E + F = 2), any closed cage of trivalent carbon atoms built from pentagons and hexagons must contain **exactly 12 pentagons. Truncated icosahedral buckminsterfullerene C60** (V=60, E=90, F=32) comprises 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Carbon nanostructure (1=Graphene 2D, 2=Nanotube CNT, 3=Fullerene C₆₀): 1 … 3, standard value 3
  • Nanotube chirality index n in (n, 0): 4 … 10, standard value 6
  • Applied bias voltage U (π-electron drift): 0 … 4 V, standard value 1.5 V
  • Lattice temperature T (phonon vibrations): 100 … 900 K, standard value 300 K
Physical model and application
Graphene is a one-atom-thick honeycomb sheet where π-electrons race like massless particles. Roll that sheet into a cylinder to get a carbon nanotube, or stitch in 12 pentagons to close it into a C60 soccer-ball cage.
