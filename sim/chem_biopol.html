Biopolymers: Peptide Bond Planarity, Protein α-Helix & DNA Double Helix (A=T, G≡C)

"It has not escaped our notice that the specific pairing we have postulated immediately suggests a possible copying mechanism for the genetic material.", James D. Watson & Francis H. C. Crick, 1953

Question
Why does a DNA double helix with a higher guanine-cytosine (G≡C) content have a significantly higher thermal denaturation (melting) temperature Tm than an A=T-rich duplex?
Selected answer Option B
Biological macromolecules rely on cooperative hydrogen bonding for their 3D secondary structure: 1) In the **Pauling-Corey protein α-helix** (3.6 amino acid residues per turn, pitch 0.54 nm), each backbone carbonyl C=O of residue i forms an intramolecular H-bond with the amide N-H of residue i+4, while the peptide bond -CO-NH- remains rigid and planar due to sp2 resonance. 2) In Watson-Crick B-DNA (10 bp/turn, pitch 3.4 nm), two antiparallel polynucleotide strands pair via complementary purine-pyrimidine H-bonds: **A=T (2 H-bonds) and G≡C (3 H-bonds)**, obeying Chargaff's rule ([A]=[T], [G]=[C]).

Advanced Level · AP / University · Biochemistry: Proteins & DNA
Mechanism

Biopolymers (proteins and nucleic acids) fold into regular 3D secondary structures stabilized by cooperative networks of directional hydrogen bonds.

Equations and conservation laws

In the **Pauling-Corey protein α-helix** (3.6 residues/turn, pitch 0.54 nm), planar trans peptide bonds (-CO-NH-) coil into a right-handed helix where every backbone C=O of residue i hydrogen-bonds to the amide N-H of residue i+4.

Measurements and applications

In Watson-Crick B-DNA (10 bp/turn, pitch 3.4 nm), two antiparallel sugar-phosphate strands pair via complementary base hydrogen bonding obeying Chargaff's rules: **Adenine-Thymine (A=T) forms 2 H-bonds, whereas Guanine-Cytosine (G≡C) forms 3 H-bonds**. Consequently, the thermal melting temperature Tm increases linearly with %GC content: Tm ≈ 69.3 + 0.41(%GC).

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Biopolymer (1=Protein α-Helix, 2=B-DNA Double Helix): 1 … 2, standard value 2
  • G≡C base-pair content (% G≡C, 3 H-bonds) / stability: 10 … 90 %, standard value 50 %
  • Number of biopolymer helix turns Nturns: 2 … 6 turns, standard value 4 turns
  • Solution temperature t (thermal denaturation above Tm): 20 … 95 °C, standard value 37 °C
Physical model and application
The stability of the DNA double helix is governed by Watson-Crick hydrogen bonding and base-stacking interactions: A=T pairs form 2 hydrogen bonds whereas G≡C pairs form 3 hydrogen bonds, causing the thermal melting temperature Tm to increase linearly with GC content.
