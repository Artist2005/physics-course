Born–Haber Thermodynamic Cycle & Ionic Lattice Energy UL

Although transferring an electron from an isolated sodium atom to chlorine is endothermic, the enormous electrostatic energy released upon assembling the 3D ionic lattice makes salt formation strongly exothermic.

Question
Why cannot the ionic lattice enthalpy Ulat of a crystal like NaCl(s) be measured directly in a single calorimeter run, and how is it determined via the Born-Haber cycle?
Selected answer Option B
Because gaseous ions (M+(g) + X-(g)) cannot be weighed into a calorimeter under standard conditions, Born and Haber applied Hess's Law to construct a closed thermodynamic cycle: Δ Hf° = Δ Hsub + (1)/(2)D(X2) + I1(M) + Eea(X) - Ulat. The endothermic steps of atomization and ionization are more than compensated by the huge exothermic electrostatic attraction when gaseous ions condense into the 3D crystal lattice (Ulat ∝ (NA A z+ z- e2)/(4πε0 r0)).

Mechanism

The Born-Haber cycle applies Hess's Law to relate the standard enthalpy of formation Δ Hf° of an ionic solid MX(s) to its lattice energy Ulat by constructing a closed thermodynamic path through gaseous atoms and ions.

Equations and conservation laws

Converting standard elements into gaseous ions requires endothermic sublimation (Δ Hsub), bond dissociation ((1)/(2)DX2), and ionization (I1), partially offset by the exothermic electron affinity (Eea) of the nonmetal.

Measurements and applications

The overall formation of the salt is strongly exothermic because condensing gaseous ions into a 3D crystal lattice releases a massive lattice enthalpy Ulat = Δ Hsub + (1)/(2)DX2 + I1 + Eea - Δ Hf°, which scales with ionic charges and inverse interionic distance according to the Born-Landé equation.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Metal sublimation enthalpy ΔHsub(M): 75 … 180 kJ/mol, standard value 108 kJ/mol
  • Halogen dissociation enthalpy ½D(X₂): 75 … 160 kJ/mol, standard value 121 kJ/mol
  • Metal first ionization energy I₁(M → M⁺): 375 … 600 kJ/mol, standard value 496 kJ/mol
  • Crystal lattice energy Ulat(M⁺ + X⁻ → MX): 650 … 1050 kJ/mol, standard value 787 kJ/mol
Physical model and application
The Born-Haber cycle applies Hess's law to relate the standard enthalpy of formation of an ionic solid (NaCl) to its elementary thermodynamic steps: endothermic energy inputs for metal sublimation, halogen bond dissociation, and ionization are more than offset by the large exothermic lattice energy release Ulat when gaseous Na+ and Cl- ions condense into a crystal.
