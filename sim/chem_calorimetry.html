Thermochemistry, Hess’s Law & Calorimetric Enthalpy Determination ΔH

The heat of a chemical reaction at constant pressure equals the enthalpy change ΔH and depends solely on the initial and final states (Hess's Law).

Core Curriculum · Grade 9-11 · Thermochemistry
Mechanism

Thermochemistry quantifies the heat exchanged during chemical reactions. At constant pressure, the heat of reaction equals the change in enthalpy Δ H = Hproducts - Hreactants. In an exothermic reaction (Δ H 0 into the surroundings (e.g., strong acid-base neutralization H+ + OH- → H2O, Δ H° = -57.3 kJ/mol). In an endothermic process (Δ H > 0), heat is absorbed from the solution (e.g., dissolution of NH4NO3, Δ H° = +25.7 kJ/mol).

Equations and conservation laws

In an insulated calorimeter, the heat balance yields Δ H = -(Qcal)/(ν) = -((msol cp + Ccal)Δ T)/(ν), where cp ≈ 4.184 J/(g·K) is the specific heat capacity of the aqueous solution and Ccal is the calorimeter constant.

Measurements and applications

Hess's Law states that because enthalpy H is a state function, the overall enthalpy change Δ H°rxn of a reaction is independent of the pathway or number of intermediate steps: Δ H°rxn = ∑ νi Δ H°f,prod - ∑ νj Δ H°f,react.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Reaction (1=HCl+NaOH exo, 2=NH₄NO₃ endo, 3=Mg+2HCl strong exo): 1 … 3, standard value 1
  • Reactant molar amount ν: 0.02 … 0.25 mol, standard value 0.1 mol
  • Calorimeter aqueous solution mass m: 100 … 400 g, standard value 200 g
  • Calorimeter vessel heat capacity Ccal: 10 … 120 J/K, standard value 45 J/K
Physical model and application
A chemical hand-warmer releases heat in an exothermic reaction (Δ H 0, temperature drops).
