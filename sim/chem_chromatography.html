Chromatography (TLC & Column): Partition Coefficient K_d & Retention Factor R_f

«Like rays of light in a spectrum, the components of a pigment mixture separate systematically across the adsorbent layer.», Mikhail Tsvet, 1903

Advanced Level · AP / University · Separation Science
Mechanism

Chromatography separates chemical mixtures by continuously partitioning analyte molecules between a stationary phase (such as polar silica gel SiO₂ coated on a TLC plate or packed in a column) and a moving mobile phase (eluent).

Equations and conservation laws

On polar silica gel, polar analytes form strong hydrogen bonds and dipole interactions with surface silanol (–Si–OH) groups (large partition coefficient K_d) and migrate slowly, whereas non-polar compounds remain in the mobile phase and travel rapidly near the solvent front.

Measurements and applications

Each component is identified by its dimensionless retention factor R_f = (zₛₚₒₜ)/(z_front) = (1)/(1 + k′), the ratio of the spot’s migration distance zₛₚₒₜ to the total distance traveled by the solvent front z_front.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Mobile phase (eluent) polarity ε°: 0.15 … 0.95, standard value 0.55
  • Solvent front migration height z_front: 2 … 9.5 cm, standard value 7.5 cm
  • Silica gel stationary phase activity: 0.6 … 2, standard value 1.2
  • Applied sample spot volume V_sample: 0.5 … 3 μL, standard value 1.5 μL
Physical model and application
Chromatographic separation on polar silica gel relies on differential partitioning between the stationary polar phase and the mobile non-polar eluent: non-polar β-carotene interacts weakly with silica and migrates near the solvent front (R_f ≈ 0.9), whereas polar chlorophyll b hydrogen-bonds strongly to surface silanol groups and elutes much more slowly (R_f ≈ 0.25).
R_f = (z_i)/(z_front) = (1)/(1 + k_i), K_d = (Cₛₜₐₜ)/(C_mob), R_s = (2(z₂ - z₁))/(w₁ + w₂)
Question
On a polar silica gel (SiO₂· xH₂O) thin-layer chromatography (TLC) plate, why do non-polar β-carotene molecules travel near the solvent front (R_f ≈ 0.90) while polar chlorophyll b migrates slowly (R_f ≈ 0.25)?
Selected answer Option B
Chromatographic separation relies on continuous partitioning of analytes between a stationary phase (polar silica gel SiO₂ with surface –OH groups) and a mobile eluent phase rising by capillary action. Each component i has a characteristic retention factor R_f,i = (z_i)/(z_front) = (1)/(1 + k_i), where z_i is the distance traveled by spot i and z_front is the solvent front height. Increasing eluent polarity increases elution strength ε°, raising R_f for all components.
