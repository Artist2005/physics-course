Colloidal Systems, Micelle Structure, Tyndall Cone & Schulze–Hardy Coagulation

"Between the realm of true molecular solutions and coarse suspensions lies the world of neglected dimensions, the colloidal state.", Wolfgang Ostwald, 1914

Question
How can a colloidal sol (particle size 1-100 nm) be distinguished optically from a true solution, and why is Al3+ (z=3) hundreds of times more effective at coagulating a negatively charged sol than Na+ (z=1)?
Selected answer Option B
Colloidal micelles (1-100 nm, such as gold sol or Fe(OH)3 sol) are stabilized against van der Waals attraction by electrostatic repulsion of their electrical double layers, characterized by the zeta potential ζ. When a laser beam passes through the sol, particles scatter light with Rayleigh intensity Iscat ∝ (N d6)/(λ4), forming a glowing Tyndall cone. Adding an electrolyte compresses the diffuse double layer; by the Schulze-Hardy rule (derived from DLVO theory), the critical coagulation concentration drops as CCC ∝ (1)/(z6) (Na+ : Ca2+ : Al3+ ≈ 1 : 64 : 729).

Mechanism

Colloidal sols contain dispersed nanoparticles or micelles ranging from **1 to 100 nm** in diameter. Unlike true solutions (< 1 nm), colloids scatter an incident light beam into a visible Tyndall cone via Rayleigh scattering (Iscat ∝ N d6 / λ4).

Equations and conservation laws

Colloidal stability is governed by DLVO theory: micelles remain dispersed as long as electrostatic repulsion between their electrical double layers (measured by the **zeta potential ζ**) outweighs short-range van der Waals attraction.

Measurements and applications

Adding an electrolyte compresses the double layer and triggers coagulation (flocculation). According to the Schulze-Hardy rule, the critical coagulation concentration (CCC) drops inversely with the sixth power of the counter-ion charge: CCC ∝ z-6 (Na+ : Ca2+ : Al3+ = 1 : 64-1 : 729-1).

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Colloidal micelle core diameter d: 2 … 80 nm, standard value 35 nm
  • Coagulating electrolyte concentration C: 0 … 15 mmol/L, standard value 2 mmol/L
  • Coagulating counter-ion charge z (1=Na⁺, 2=Ca²⁺, 3=Al³⁺): 1 … 3, standard value 1
  • Laser beam wavelength λ: 405 … 650 nm, standard value 532 nm
Physical model and application
A light beam passing through a colloidal sol forms a visible Tyndall cone due to Rayleigh-Tyndall scattering by dispersed nanoparticles. Electrolyte-induced coagulation of charged micelles obeys the Schulze-Hardy rule: critical coagulation power scales with the sixth power of counter-ion charge (z6), making trivalent Al3+ 36 = 729 times more effective than Na+.
