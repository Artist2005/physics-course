Electrochemical Corrosion of Metals & Sacrificial Anode Cathodic Protection

«Attach a small block of zinc or iron to the copper sheathing of a ship, and the sea water will no longer corrode the protected metal.», Humphry Davy, 1824

Core Curriculum · Grade 9–11 · Corrosion & Protection
Mechanism

Electrochemical corrosion destroys metals via short-circuited microgalvanic cells beneath an aqueous electrolyte film. In any bimetallic couple, the metal with the more negative standard reduction potential E° acts as the anode (-) and dissolves (M → Mⁿ⁺ + ne⁻), while dissolved oxygen is reduced at the cathode (+): O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻.

Equations and conservation laws

Because E°(Fe²⁺/Fe) = -0.44 V, coupling steel with a nobler metal such as tin (-0.14 V) or copper (+0.34 V) makes iron the anode, severely accelerating rusting at any coating scratch.

Measurements and applications

In sacrificial cathodic protection (galvanized steel or hull anodes), iron is coupled to a more active metal such as zinc (E° = -0.76 V). Zinc serves as the sacrificial anode (Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻), pumping electrons into the steel cathode and preventing iron oxidation even if the coating is breached.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Contact metal coupled to Fe (1=Cu, 2=Sn, 3=Zn protector, 4=Mg protector): 1 … 4, standard value 1
  • Electrolyte salinity [NaCl]: 0.05 … 1.5 mol/L, standard value 0.5 mol/L
  • Dissolved oxygen concentration [O₂]: 1 … 12 mg/L, standard value 8 mg/L
  • Medium acidity pH: 3 … 10 pH, standard value 7 pH
Physical model and application
Sacrificial cathodic protection of steel structures relies on standard electrode potentials: because zinc (E° = -0.76 V) is more active than iron (E° = -0.44 V), a zinc coating acts as the sacrificial anode and feeds electrons to protect exposed steel, whereas contact with copper (E° = +0.34 V) makes iron the corroding anode.
Anode (-): Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻, Cathode (+): O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻
Question
Why does a scratch in a zinc coating (galvanized steel, E°_Zn = -0.76 V) still protect the underlying iron (E°_Fe = -0.44 V) from rusting, whereas a scratch in a tin or copper coating (E°_Cu = +0.34 V) drastically accelerates iron corrosion?
Selected answer Option B
Electrochemical corrosion operates as a short-circuited microgalvanic cell on the metal surface beneath an electrolyte film. The metal with the lower electrode potential serves as the anode (oxidation) while oxygen reduction (O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻) or proton reduction (2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂↑) occurs at the cathode. Because E°(Zn²⁺/Zn) = -0.76 V < E°(Fe²⁺/Fe) = -0.44 V, a zinc sacrificial protector oxidizes (Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻) and feeds electrons to the iron hull, completely suppressing rust formation.
