Electronegativity, Bond Polarity & Molecular Dipole Moment Vector μ

The dipole moment of a molecule is its 3D geometry multiplied by the asymmetry of its electron cloud.

Question
Why does the bent water molecule H2O (θ = 104.5°) possess a large permanent dipole moment (μ = 1.85 D), whereas linear carbon dioxide CO2 (θ = 180°) is nonpolar (μnet = 0)?
Selected answer Option B
A difference in Pauling electronegativity Δχ = |χA - χB| shifts shared electron density toward the more electronegative atom, creating partial charges ±δ q and a bond dipole vector μbond = δ q · d (1 D = 3.336× 10-30 C·m). For a symmetrical triatomic molecule AB2 with bond angle θ, vector addition gives μnet = 2μbond cos (θ/2). When θ = 180° (CO2, BeCl2), the two equal and opposite bond dipoles cancel (μnet = 0).
Formula
μbond = δ q · d, μnet = ∑i μi ⇒ μnet = 2μbond cos ((θ)/(2)), τ = μ×E

Core Curriculum · Grade 8-10 · Molecular Polarity
Mechanism

When two bonded atoms differ in Pauling electronegativity (Δχ = |χA - χB| > 0), shared electron density shifts toward the more electronegative atom, generating partial charges ±δ q and a bond dipole vector μbond = δ q d (1 D = 3.336× 10-30 C·m).

Equations and conservation laws

The net polarity of a polyatomic molecule is the vector sum of its individual bond dipoles: μnet = ∑i μi. For a triatomic AB2 molecule with bond angle θ, μnet = 2μbond cos (θ/2).

Measurements and applications

Consequently, bent H2O (θ = 104.5°) has a large permanent dipole (1.85 D) and experiences an aligning torque τ = μ×E in an external electric field, whereas linear CO2 (θ = 180°) has μnet = 0 because its two polar C=O vectors cancel.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Pauling electronegativity difference Δχ = |χB − χA|: 0 … 2.5, standard value 1.24
  • Valence bond angle θ (105° = H₂O, 180° = CO₂): 90 … 180 °, standard value 105 °
  • Interatomic bond length l(A-B): 0.9 … 1.8 Å, standard value 0.96 Å
  • External electric field strength E: 0 … 5 kV/cm, standard value 2 kV/cm
Physical model and application
The net polarity of a polyatomic molecule equals the vector sum of its individual bond dipoles. In linear CO2 (180°), two equal and opposite C=O bond dipoles cancel completely (μ = 0), whereas in bent H2O (104.5°) the two O-H dipoles add vectorially to produce a strong permanent dipole.
