Industrial Ammonia Synthesis (Haber–Bosch Process): N₂ + 3H₂ ⇄ 2NH₃

Bread from air: cleaving the triple bond of nitrogen N≡N required uniting high-pressure chemical thermodynamics with heterogeneous iron catalysis.

Question
Why is industrial ammonia synthesis (N2 + 3H2 ⇄ 2NH3, Δ H° = -92.4 kJ/mol) operated at a compromise temperature of 400-500 °C and high pressure (150-300 atm) over an iron catalyst?
Selected answer Option B
The reaction N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇄ 2NH3(g) (Δ H° = -92.4 kJ/mol) reduces the number of gas molecules from 4 to 2 (Δ ngas = -2). By Le Chatelier's principle, high pressure (150-300 atm) strongly increases equilibrium NH3 yield. Although lower temperature favors the exothermic equilibrium, the N≡N triple bond (945 kJ/mol) is kinetically inert at room temperature. A promoted porous **iron (Fe) catalyst** dissociatively chemisorbs N2 and H2, allowing rapid synthesis at the industrial compromise temperature of 400-500 °C.

Mechanism

Ammonia synthesis demands a strict engineering balance. In the Haber-Bosch process (N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇄ 2NH3(g), Δ H° = -92.4 kJ/mol), thermodynamic equilibrium yield and reaction rate pull in opposite temperature directions.

Equations and conservation laws

Because the forward reaction is exothermic and reduces gas mole count from 4 to 2, Le Chatelier's principle dictates that equilibrium yield ηeq(NH3) is maximized at high pressure and low temperature. However, at low temperatures the reaction is kinetically frozen by the immense dissociation energy of the N≡N triple bond (945 kJ/mol).

Measurements and applications

Industrial reactors therefore operate at a kinetic-thermodynamic compromise of 400-500 °C and 150-300 atm over a promoted porous **iron (Fe) catalyst** that lowers the activation barrier via dissociative chemisorption.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Synthesis column pressure P: 50 … 350 atm, standard value 200 atm
  • Catalyst bed temperature t: 300 … 600 °C, standard value 450 °C
  • Promoted Fe catalyst activity: 0 … 1, standard value 1
  • Feed stoichiometric ratio [H₂]:[N₂]: 1 … 4, standard value 3
Physical model and application
Dinitrogen N≡N is locked by a triple bond: too cold and nothing reacts, too hot and exothermic NH3 decomposes back. Haber and Bosch solved the dilemma with high pressure (200 atm), 450 °C, and a porous iron catalyst.
