Hydrogen Bonding, Hexagonal Ice Iₕ Lattice & Density Anomaly of Water

Each water molecule forms four directional tetrahedral hydrogen bonds (109.5°), locking ice Iₕ into an open hexagonal cage less dense than liquid water.

Question
Why is the density of crystalline ice Ih (0.917 g/cm3) lower than that of liquid water (1.000 g/cm3 at +4 °C), causing ice to float on lakes and oceans?
Selected answer Option B
With two O-H covalent bonds and two lone pairs on oxygen (sp3), each molecule in ice Ih forms 4 directional hydrogen bonds at tetrahedral angles (109.5°), creating open six-membered hexagonal channels (ρ = 0.917 g/cm3). Upon melting at 0 °C, partial collapse of these open rings increases packing density up to 1.000 g/cm3 at +4 °C.

Core Curriculum · Grade 9-10 · Hydrogen Bonding
Mechanism

A hydrogen bond (O-H·sO) is a directional intermolecular attraction between a partially positive hydrogen atom (δ+) covalently bonded to a strongly electronegative atom (O, N, F) and a lone electron pair on an adjacent electronegative atom (δ-). In water, EHB ≈ 21 kJ/mol, much stronger than van der Waals dispersion (2-5 kJ/mol) though weaker than the covalent O-H bond (463 kJ/mol).

Equations and conservation laws

Because each H2O molecule possesses two O-H donors and two lone-pair acceptors on its sp3 oxygen atom, crystalline hexagonal ice **Ih locks every molecule into a strict 4-coordinate tetrahedral cage** (109.5°). This creates open six-membered hexagonal channels with large empty voids, giving ice a low density of 0.917 g/cm3.

Measurements and applications

Upon melting at 0 °C, roughly 15% of the hydrogen bonds break, allowing molecules to pack into the collapsed cavities and raising the density to a maximum of 1.000 g/cm3 at +4 °C. This density anomaly ensures that ice floats and insulates aquatic ecosystems in winter.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Medium temperature t (phase transition at 0 °C): -25 … 60 °C, standard value -5 °C
  • Hydrogen bond energy EHB (O-H···O): 10 … 30 kJ/mol, standard value 21 kJ/mol
  • External pressure P (hexagonal cage compression): 0.1 … 200 MPa, standard value 0.1 MPa
  • Water molecular dipole moment μ(H₂O): 1 … 2.5 D, standard value 1.85 D
Physical model and application
In liquid water molecules crowd closely together, but upon freezing into ice Ih each H2O extends four rigid tetrahedral arms (hydrogen bonds) to form open hexagonal honeycombs with empty channels, making ice less dense than liquid water.
