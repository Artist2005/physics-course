Ionic Crystal Lattice & Lattice Energy (NaCl)

In an ionic crystal, every cation is octahedrally coordinated by six anions, forming a continuous three-dimensional electrostatic lattice.

Question
How do the crystal lattice energy |Ulat| and melting point of an ionic solid change when ionic charges (z+, z-) increase or interionic distance r0 decreases?
Selected answer Option B
By the Born-Landé equation, ionic lattice energy is directly proportional to the charge product z+ z- and inversely proportional to the interionic distance r0 = r+ + r-. Moving from monovalent Na+Cl- (Ulat ≈ 787 kJ/mol, Tm = 801 °C) to divalent Mg2+O2- nearly quadruples lattice energy (3790 kJ/mol, Tm = 2852 °C).

Core Curriculum · Grade 8-11
Mechanism

Crystalline solids possess long-range periodic order across a 3D lattice. In ionic crystals (such as NaCl, face-centered cubic rocksalt lattice with 6:6 coordination), cations and anions are bound by omnidirectional electrostatic attraction. In covalent network crystals (diamond, quartz SiO2), atoms form a continuous 3D scaffold of rigid directional bonds (sp3 tetrahedra in diamond). In metallic crystals, positive ion cores are immersed in a mobile, delocalized Fermi sea of conduction electrons.

Equations and conservation laws

The cohesive energy of an ionic crystal is given by the Born-Landé equation: U0 = -(NA M |z+ z-| e2)/(4πε0 r0)(1 - (1)/(nB)), where M is the Madelung constant capturing the geometric lattice sum of electrostatic interactions (M = 1.7476 for NaCl), r0 is the equilibrium interionic distance, and nB ≈ 8-12 characterizes short-range Born repulsion between closed electron shells.

Measurements and applications

Increasing absolute temperature T amplifies the root-mean-square thermal vibration amplitude of lattice sites ⟨ u2⟩ ∝ (kB T)/(kbond). By Lindemann's melting criterion, when the thermal vibration amplitude reaches ≈ 10-15% of the interatomic spacing r0, long-range order breaks down and the crystal melts. Hence crystals with high ionic charges and short bond lengths (MgO, Al2O3, diamond) exhibit exceptionally high melting points.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Interionic distance r₀ (r₊ + r₋): 2.1 … 3.5 Å, standard value 2.82 Å
  • Ion charge magnitude |z±| (1=NaCl, 2=MgO): 1 … 2 e, standard value 1 e
  • Crystal temperature T: 100 … 1000 K, standard value 300 K
  • Born repulsion exponent n: 6 … 12, standard value 9
Physical model and application
Crystal lattice architecture governs bulk material properties: table salt NaCl alternates Na+ and Cl- ions in a cubic ionic lattice, whereas diamond links every sp3 carbon atom to four neighbors via rigid covalent bonds, forming an ultrahard 3D network.
