Molecular Orbitals (LCAO–MO): Bonding σ/π, Antibonding σ*/π* & Bond Order

An atomic orbital is monocentric, whereas a molecular orbital embraces all nuclei of the molecule as a single quantum wave.

Question
In Molecular Orbital (MO) theory, how is the bond order B defined, and why does the oxygen molecule O2 exhibit paramagnetism (attraction into a magnetic field)?
Selected answer Option B
Constructive interference of atomic wavefunctions (ψ+ = c(ψA + ψB)) builds electron density between nuclei, forming a lower-energy bonding MO (σ, π). Destructive interference (ψ- = c(ψA - ψB)) creates a nodal plane between nuclei and a higher-energy antibonding MO (σ^*, π^*). In O2 (12 valence electrons), the last two electrons singly occupy the degenerate π2p,x^* and π2p,y^* orbitals with parallel spins (S = 1), yielding bond order B = (1)/(2)(8 - 4) = 2.0 and strong paramagnetism.

Mechanism

The Molecular Orbital (LCAO-MO) theory treats valence electrons as delocalized over the entire molecule via linear combinations of atomic orbitals ψA and ψB. Constructive interference (ψ+ = c(ψA + ψB)) accumulates electron density between the nuclei, forming a lower-energy bonding MO (σg, πu). Destructive interference (ψ- = c(ψA - ψB)) creates a nodal plane between the nuclei, forming a higher-energy antibonding MO (σu^*, πg^*).

Equations and conservation laws

Chemical bond order is defined as B = (1)/(2)(Nbond - Nanti). For He2, B = (1)/(2)(2 - 2) = 0 (unstable), whereas for dinitrogen N2, B = (1)/(2)(8 - 2) = 3.0 (D0 = 945 kJ/mol).

Measurements and applications

MO theory triumphantly explains why liquid and gaseous **dioxygen O2 is paramagnetic**: of its 12 valence electrons, the highest two occupy the degenerate antibonding π2p,x^* and π2p,y^* orbitals singly with parallel spins (Hund's rule, S = 1, μs = 2.83 μB), giving B = 2.0.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Diatomic species (1=H₂, 2=He₂⁺, 3=N₂, 4=O₂, 5=F₂): 1 … 5, standard value 4
  • Internuclear distance R: 0.7 … 2.2 Å, standard value 1.21 Å
  • Displayed MO (1=σg bonding, 2=σ*_u, 3=πu, 4=π*_g): 1 … 4, standard value 1
  • Resonance overlap integral |β|: 1 … 4.5 eV, standard value 2.5 eV
Physical model and application
By the linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO), constructive in-phase overlap of atomic wavefunctions concentrates electron density between the nuclei to stabilize the bond (bonding MO σ, π), whereas destructive out-of-phase overlap creates a nodal plane between the nuclei and raises energy (antibonding MO σ^*, π^*).
