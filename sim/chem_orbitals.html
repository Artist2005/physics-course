Atomic Electron Shells & 3D Quantum Orbitals (s, p, d)

An electron in an atom does not follow a classical trajectory; instead, it forms a 3D probability density cloud |ψ(r,θ,φ)|² governed by quantum numbers n, l, ml.

Question
What determines the geometric shape of an atomic orbital (spherical s, dumbbell p, four-lobed d) and the total number of nodal surfaces where |ψ|2 = 0?
Selected answer Option B
Solving Schrödinger's equation in spherical coordinates factors into a radial wavefunction Rn,l(r) and spherical harmonics Ylm(θ,φ). The azimuthal quantum number l sets the angular symmetry and number of nodal planes (l), while the principal quantum number n sets the energy level En and total nodal count n - 1.

Core Curriculum · Grade 8-11
Mechanism

In quantum mechanics, the state of an electron in the Coulomb field of a nucleus is governed by the time-independent Schrödinger equation Hψn,l,ml(r,θ,φ) = Enψn,l,ml(r,θ,φ). The wave function separates into a radial component Rn,l(r) and a spherical harmonic Yl,ml(θ,φ). By Born's statistical interpretation, |ψ(r,θ,φ)|2 dV gives the probability of finding the electron within volume element dV.

Equations and conservation laws

Three quantum numbers uniquely specify the spatial orbital: the principal quantum number n = 1, 2, 3, … determines the energy level En = -(Zeff2· 13.6 eV)/(n2) and shell radius; the azimuthal (orbital) quantum number l = 0, 1, …, n-1 fixes the angular momentum L = ℏ√(l(l+1)) and shape (l=0 spherical s-orbital, l=1 dumbbell p-orbital, l=2 cloverleaf d-orbital); and the magnetic quantum number ml ∈ [-l, +l] sets the spatial orientation.

Measurements and applications

The total number of nodal surfaces where probability density vanishes (|ψ|2 = 0) is Nnodes = n - 1. Exactly l of these are angular nodes (planes or cones passing through the nucleus that carve the orbital into lobes), while the remaining n - l - 1 are radial spherical nodes separating concentric shells of electron density.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Principal quantum number n (shell): 1 … 3, standard value 2
  • Orbital quantum number l (0=s, 1=p, 2=d): 0 … 2, standard value 1
  • Effective nuclear charge Zeff: 1 … 3 e, standard value 1 e
  • 3D cloud density scale |ψ|²: 0.6 … 1.5 ×, standard value 1 ×
Physical model and application
In the quantum-mechanical model of the atom, classical trajectories are replaced by three-dimensional probability density clouds |ψ|2: s-orbitals are spherically symmetric, p-orbitals form two-lobed dumbbells divided by a nodal plane, and d-orbitals exhibit four-lobed cloverleaf geometries.
