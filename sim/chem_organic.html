Benzene Structure C₆H₆, Aromaticity & Delocalized π-Bonding

Six unhybridized 2pz orbitals on the carbon ring overlap continuously above and below the molecular plane, forming a unified 6π aromatic electron cloud.

Question
Why do all six Carbon-Carbon bonds in benzene C6H6 have the exact same intermediate length of 1.40 Å (between a single 1.54 Å and double 1.34 Å bond), making benzene far more stable than alkenes?
Selected answer Option B
Each carbon atom in benzene C6H6 is sp2-hybridized in a planar regular hexagon (120°). The six perpendicular 2pz electrons form a continuous cyclic π cloud (4n+2 = 6 for n=1), equalizing every C-C bond order to 1.5 (1.40 Å) and conferring ≈ 150 kJ/mol of aromatic resonance stabilization.

Core Curriculum · Grade 9-11 · Organic Chemistry
Mechanism

The vast structural diversity of organic molecules arises from carbon's ability (1s2 2s2 2p2) to adopt three distinct valence hybridization states. In alkanes (ethane C2H6), carbon atoms are **sp3-hybridized** with tetrahedral bond angles of 109.5° and single σ-bonds of length 1.54 Å, permitting internal rotation between staggered and eclipsed conformations.

Equations and conservation laws

In alkenes (ethene / ethylene C2H4), carbon atoms are **sp2-hybridized** in a trigonal planar geometry (120°). The C=C double bond (1.34 Å) comprises one axial σ-bond and one lateral π-bond formed by side-by-side overlap of unhybridized 2pz orbitals above and below the molecular plane, locking rotation.

Measurements and applications

In benzene C6H6, six sp2 carbon atoms form a flat regular hexagon (120°). Their six unhybridized 2pz electrons overlap continuously around the ring to form a **delocalized aromatic π-system** consisting of two toroidal electron clouds above and below the ring plane (satisfying Hückel's 4n+2 = 6 rule). Consequently, all six C-C bonds are identical with an intermediate bond order of 1.5 and length 1.40 Å, conferring 150 kJ/mol of resonance stabilization.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • π-delocalization degree (0=Kekulé triene, 1=benzene): 0 … 1, standard value 1
  • External magnetic field B₀ (induced π-ring current): 0 … 3 T, standard value 1.5 T
  • Aromatic ring plane tilt angle: 0 … 45 °, standard value 18 °
  • Ring vibrational amplitude: 0 … 1 ×, standard value 0.4 ×
Physical model and application
The spatial geometry and reactivity of hydrocarbons are governed by carbon hybridization: in ethane C2H6 (sp3), two tetrahedral methyl groups rotate freely around a single σ-bond; in ethene C2H4 (sp2), lateral pz overlap in the double (σ+π) bond locks the molecule into a plane; and in benzene C6H6, six π-electrons delocalize into continuous aromatic clouds above and below the hexagonal ring.
