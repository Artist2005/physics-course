Periodic Table of Elements (118 Elements): Atomic Radius rₐt, Ionization I₁ & χ

Moving left to right across a period, effective nuclear charge Zeff = Z − σ rises steadily, contracting the valence shell, while stepping down to a new period adds a new principal electron shell.

Question
How do atomic radius rat, first ionization energy I1, and electronegativity χ change as you move from left to right across a single period of the Periodic Table?
Selected answer Option B
Across a period, electrons enter the same outer shell n and shield one another imperfectly, so Slater's effective nuclear charge Zeff = Z - σ increases. Stronger Coulomb attraction contracts the valence shell (rat decreases, including d-block and 4f lanthanide contraction) and binds valence electrons more tightly (I1 and χ increase).
Formula
Zeff = Z - σ, rat ≈ (n2 a0)/(Zeff), I1 ∝ (Zeff2)/(n2)

Core Curriculum · Grade 8-11 · Periodic Trends
Mechanism

In modern quantum mechanics, the Periodic Law states that the physical and chemical properties of elements vary periodically with their **atomic number Z**, driven by the periodic recurrence of outer-shell valence electron configurations (ns1 … ns2 np6).

Equations and conservation laws

The governing parameter behind atomic radius rat, first ionization energy I1, and Pauling electronegativity χ is Slater's effective nuclear charge Zeff = Z - σ, where σ is the shielding constant of core electrons. Moving left to right across a period (n = const), electrons enter the same principal shell and shield one another poorly (Δ σ ≈ 0.35), causing Zeff to increase by ≈ 0.65 per step. Stronger electrostatic attraction pulls the valence shell inward (rat ↓) while increasing ionization energy I1 and electronegativity χ.

Measurements and applications

Descending a main group adds new electron shells (n = 1, 2, 3, 4…). Atomic radius rat grows, I1 and χ drop, and alkali metal reactivity rises sharply from Li through K to Cs.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Atomic number Z (all 118 elements): 1 … 118, standard value 26
  • Period n (1-7, electron shells): 1 … 7, standard value 4
  • IUPAC Group (1-18: s-, d-, p-blocks): 1 … 18, standard value 8
  • 3D matrix relief (1=Radius rat, 2=Ionization I₁, 3=Electroneg. χ): 1 … 3, standard value 1
Physical model and application
Moving left to right across a period is like strengthening a central magnet while keeping the same shell ring: higher effective nuclear charge Zeff pulls the shell inward (smaller atomic radius) and holds electrons more tightly (higher ionization energy).
