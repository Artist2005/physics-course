Phase Diagrams P–T, Triple Point, Critical Point & Supercritical Fluid

Gibbs' phase rule establishes nature's strict constitution: how many phases may coexist in equilibrium at any given temperature and pressure.

Question
What is unique about the triple point (Tt, Pt) and critical point (Tc, Pc) on a single-component P-T phase diagram, and why does the fusion curve of water have a negative slope dP/dT < 0?
Selected answer Option B
By Gibbs' Phase Rule (f = C - P + 2 = 3 - P), three phases coexist only at a single invariant triple point (f = 0). For CO2, Pt = 5.11 atm > 1 atm, so at atmospheric pressure solid dry ice sublimes directly into gas at -78.5 °C. Above the critical point (T > Tc, P > Pc), the density of gas equals that of liquid, the meniscus disappears, and the substance forms a supercritical fluid. For water, ice is less dense than liquid water (Δ Vfus < 0), giving a negative Clapeyron melting slope dP/dT < 0.

Mechanism

A single-component **P-T phase diagram maps the stability fields of solid, liquid, and gas separated by coexistence curves governed by the Clapeyron equation** (dP)/(dT) = (Δ Htr)/(T Δ Vtr). Because ice is less dense than liquid water (Δ Vfus < 0), water's melting curve has an anomalous negative slope (dP/dT < 0).

Equations and conservation laws

By Gibbs' Phase Rule (f = 3 - Φ), solid, liquid, and vapor coexist exclusively at the invariant triple point (Tt, Pt). Because Pt(CO2) = 5.11 atm > 1 atm, solid CO2 ("dry ice") sublimes directly into gas at atmospheric pressure.

Measurements and applications

The liquid-vapor curve ends abruptly at the critical point (Tc, Pc). Past that threshold, the meniscus disappears. The substance enters a supercritical fluid state that pairs liquid-like solvating density with gas-like diffusivity and zero surface tension.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Substance (1=H₂O water, 2=CO₂ dry ice / supercritical): 1 … 2, standard value 2
  • Autoclave temperature T: 170 … 420 K, standard value 260 K
  • External chamber pressure P: 1 … 90 atm, standard value 15 atm
  • Thermal heat exchange power Q: 0.5 … 5 kJ, standard value 2 kJ
Physical model and application
Why do ice skates glide on a thin water film while dry ice (CO2) smokes without melting into a puddle? Because water expands on freezing (dP/dT < 0), and CO2's triple point (5.11 atm) lies above atmospheric pressure.
