Raoult’s Law & Colligative Properties: Ebullioscopy ΔT_b & Cryoscopy ΔT_f

«The lowering of vapor pressure, elevation of boiling point, and depression of freezing point depend solely on the concentration of solute particles.», François-Marie Raoult, 1887

Advanced Level · AP / University · Colligative Properties
Mechanism

Colligative properties depend solely on the total concentration of dissolved solute particles (i· m, where i is the van ’t Hoff dissociation factor and m is molality) rather than their chemical identity.

Equations and conservation laws

By Raoult’s Law, a non-volatile solute reduces the surface mole fraction of solvent (χ₁ = 1 - χ₂), lowering the equilibrium vapor pressure proportionally: P = χ₁ P₀° ⇒ Δ P = P₀° - P = χ₂ P₀°.

Measurements and applications

Because the solution’s vapor pressure curve is depressed relative to pure solvent, the boiling point rises (ebullioscopy, Δ T_b = i K_b m, K_b = 0.512 K·kg/mol for water) and the freezing point drops (cryoscopy, Δ T_f = i K_f m, K_f = 1.86 K·kg/mol), explaining road de-icing salts and antifreeze coolants.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Solute molality m (moles per 1 kg H₂O): 0.5 … 5 mol/kg, standard value 2 mol/kg
  • Van ’t Hoff factor i (1=glucose, 2=NaCl, 3=CaCl₂): 1 … 3, standard value 2
  • Pure solvent vapor pressure P₀°: 3.2 … 101.3 kPa, standard value 23.8 kPa
  • Manometer bath temperature t: 25 … 100 °C, standard value 65 °C
Physical model and application
Dissolved salt ions act like friendly blockers at the liquid surface, making it harder for water molecules to escape into vapor or lock into ice. That’s why salted water boils above 100 °C and melts road ice below 0 °C.

Question
Why does spreading salt (NaCl, i ≈ 2 or CaCl₂, i ≈ 3) melt ice on winter roads, and why does an aqueous solution boil at a temperature above 100 °C?
Selected answer Option B
According to Raoult’s Law, a non-volatile solute lowers the mole fraction of solvent at the surface and reduces its chemical potential, depressing the equilibrium vapor pressure: P = χ₁ P₀° ⇒ Δ P = P₀° - P = χ₂ P₀°. Because the solution’s vapor pressure curve lies below that of pure water, a higher temperature is needed to reach 1 atm (boiling point elevation Δ T_b = i K_b m, K_b = 0.512 K·kg/mol) and a lower temperature is required to freeze (freezing point depression Δ T_f = i K_f m, K_f = 1.86 K·kg/mol).
