Core Curriculum · Grade 8-11
Mechanism

The acidity or basicity of an aqueous solution is quantified by its pH value: pH = - log 10[H3O+]. Due to water autoionization (2H2O ⇄ H3O+ + OH-), the ion-product constant of water at 25 °C is Kw = [H+][OH-] = 10-14 M2, giving pH + pOH = 14. Solutions with pH 7 basic (alkaline).

Equations and conservation laws

In an acid-base titration of a strong monoprotic acid (HCl) with a strong base (NaOH), hydronium and hydroxide ions neutralize via H+ + OH- → H2O (Δ H = -57.3 kJ/mol). The equivalence point occurs at titrant volume Veq = (Ca Va)/(Cb), where n(H+) = n(OH-). Because pH is logarithmic, adding a single drop of base (± 0.05 mL) at Veq produces a steep vertical titration jump spanning pH ≈ 4 to 10.

Measurements and applications

Acid-base indicators are weak organic acids or bases whose conjugate forms HInd and Ind- exhibit distinct colors. Phenolphthalein is colorless in acidic and neutral media (pH < 8.2) and transitions sharply to a vivid magenta-pink within pH ∈ [8.2, 10.0], falling right inside the steep equivalence jump.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Added titrant volume V(NaOH) from burette: 0 … 40 mL, standard value 18 mL
  • Initial acid concentration C(HCl) (V₀ = 25 mL): 0.05 … 0.15 mol/L, standard value 0.1 mol/L
  • Titrant base concentration C(NaOH): 0.05 … 0.15 mol/L, standard value 0.1 mol/L
  • Indicator (1=Phenolphthalein, 2=Bromothymol, 3=Methyl orange): 1 … 3, standard value 1
Physical model and application
Acid-base titration is a high-precision volumetric analysis technique. As long as unneutralized strong acid HCl remains in the receiving flask, phenolphthalein stays colorless; right at the equivalence point (Ca Va = Cb Vb), neutralizing the final H+ ions causes a steep pH jump so that a single excess drop of NaOH turns the solution vivid magenta.
