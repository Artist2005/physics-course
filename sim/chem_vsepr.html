Orbital Hybridization & 3D Molecular Geometry (VSEPR)

Valence electron pairs around a central atom arrange themselves in 3D space so as to minimize mutual Coulomb repulsion.

Question
Why are the ∠H-O-H bond angle in water H2O (104.5°) and the ∠H-N-H angle in ammonia NH3 (107.3°) smaller than the ideal tetrahedral angle of methane CH4 (109.5°)?
Selected answer Option B
In VSEPR theory, CH4, NH3, and H2O all share steric number SN = 4 (sp3 hybridization). However, a lone pair (E) sits closer to the central nucleus than a shared bonding pair (X), compressing the bond angles progressively from 109.5° → 107.3° → 104.5°.

Mechanism

The Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) model (Gillespie-Nyholm) predicts the 3D geometry of covalent molecules AXnEm. Electron domains in the valence shell of central atom A, both bonding pairs (X) and lone pairs (E), orient themselves to minimize Pauli and Coulomb repulsion by maximizing their angular separation.

Equations and conservation laws

The steric number S = n + m dictates the electron-domain geometry: S = 2 linear (180°, sp hybridization, e.g. CO2), S = 3 trigonal planar (120°, sp2, BF3), and S = 4 tetrahedral (109.5°, sp3, CH4). Because a lone pair E is localized near a single nucleus rather than stretched between two nuclei, it occupies a wider angular domain: repulsion follows E-E > E-X > X-X. Consequently, the bond angle contracts to 107.3° in NH3 (AX3E1) and 104.5° in H2O (AX2E2).

Measurements and applications

Molecular polarity is governed by the vector sum of individual bond dipoles μnet = ∑i=1n μi. In symmetric geometries without lone pairs (AX2, AX3, AX4), identical bond dipoles cancel out (|μnet| = 0, nonpolar molecule), whereas lone pairs break inversion symmetry and yield a strong permanent dipole moment (such as μ(H2O) = 1.85 D).

Physical model and application
According to VSEPR theory, valence electron domains around a central atom orient to minimize electrostatic repulsion: 180° for two domains, 120° for three, and 109.5° for a four-domain tetrahedron. Because a non-bonding lone pair E is held closer to the central nucleus, its wider charge cloud compresses adjacent bond angles.
