Compton Scattering & Relativistic Photon Momentum

The billiard-like scattering of X-ray quanta proved that a photon carries not only energy hν but also linear momentum h/λ.

Question
At what scattering angle θ is the Compton wavelength increase Δλ = λC(1 - cos θ) maximal, and what is its value?
Selected answer Option B
Since Δλ = λC(1 - cos θ) with λC = (h)/(me c) = 2.426 pm, the factor 1 - cos θ reaches its maximum value of 2 for head-on backscattering (θ = 180°), transferring maximum kinetic energy to the recoil electron.

Advanced Level · AP / University
Mechanism

In 1923, Arthur Holly Compton observed that X-rays scattered by electrons in graphite through angle θ emerge with a longer wavelength λ' > λ0, where the wavelength shift Δλ = λ' - λ0 depends solely on the scattering angle θ. Classical Thomson scattering (which predicts λ' = λ0) could not account for this shift.

Equations and conservation laws

Treating the interaction as a relativistic elastic billiard collision between a photon of energy E0 = (hc)/(λ0) and momentum p0 = (h)/(λ0) and a stationary electron of rest energy me c2 ≈ 511 keV, conservation of relativistic 4-momentum (E0 + me c2 = E' + √(pe2 c2 + me2 c4) and p0 = p' + pe) yields Compton's scattering formula: **Δλ = λ' - λ0 = λC (1 - cos θ)**.

Measurements and applications

Here λC = (h)/(me c) ≈ 2.426 pm is the Compton wavelength of the electron. Maximum energy transfer to the recoil electron occurs for 180° backscattering (cos θ = -1), where Δλmax = 2λC ≈ 4.85 pm. Compton's discovery proved conclusively that photons carry mechanical momentum p = ℏk.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Incident X-ray wavelength λ₀: 2 … 12 pm, standard value 5 pm
  • Photon scattering angle θ: 15 … 180 °, standard value 90 °
  • Target collision frequency: 0.8 … 2.4 Hz, standard value 1.4 Hz
Physical model and application
Compton scattering provides direct experimental confirmation of the corpuscular momentum of electromagnetic radiation. In an elastic collision between an X-ray photon and a free electron, kinetic energy transferred to the recoiling electron leaves the scattered photon with a reduced frequency and longer wavelength λ' > λ0.
