Coriolis Force & Motion in a Rotating Reference Frame

A body moving radially in an inertial frame traces a curved spiral in a rotating frame under the action of the Coriolis inertial force.

Question
A puck is launched radially outward from the center of a horizontal platform rotating counterclockwise (ω pointing upward). In which direction is the puck deflected relative to the rotating platform?
Selected answer Option B
In a reference frame rotating with angular velocity ω, any moving body experiences the Coriolis inertial force FCor = -2m[ω×vrel]. By the right-hand rule, when ω points upward (Northern Hemisphere / counterclockwise rotation), the Coriolis force is perpendicular to vrel and deflects the trajectory to the right of the motion direction. Since FCor ⊥ vrel, its mechanical work is zero.

Mechanism

Newton's laws take their standard form only in inertial reference frames. When observing motion from a rotating non-inertial platform with angular velocity Ω, two fictitious inertial forces appear in the rotating frame: the centrifugal force Fcf = -m[Ω × [Ω × r]] and the Coriolis force FC = -2m[Ω × vrel].

Equations and conservation laws

In the stationary laboratory frame (shown by the straight dashed reference line in 3D), a frictionless puck launched radially outward at speed vr moves in a perfect straight line. Meanwhile, the turntable rotates beneath it through angle Δφ = Ω t, so the trace scribed on the disk forms an Archimedean spiral φ(r) = -(Ω)/(vr)r with lateral deflection s ≈ Ω vr t2.

Measurements and applications

Because -2m[Ω × vrel] acts perpendicular to vrel, it performs no work (FC · vrel = 0) yet deflects trajectories to the right in the Northern Hemisphere (Ωz > 0), driving cyclonic weather systems, ocean gyres, and the precession of Foucault's pendulum.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Platform angular speed ω: -2.4 … 2.4 rad/s, standard value 1.2 rad/s
  • Launch radial speed v₀: 1.2 … 4 m/s, standard value 2.5 m/s
  • Sphere mass m: 0.5 … 3 kg, standard value 1.5 kg
  • Initial offset from center r₀: 0 … 1.5 m, standard value 0.2 m
Physical model and application
In a rotating reference frame, a body moving radially experiences a lateral deflection due to the Coriolis inertial force. On the rotating Earth, this Coriolis acceleration drives the cyclonic rotation of weather systems and precesses the swing plane of Foucault's pendulum.
