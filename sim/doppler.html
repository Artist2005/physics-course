Doppler Effect & Wavefront Deformation

A moving source crowds wavefront crests ahead of itself, shortening wavelength λ′ and raising the observed frequency.

Question
A monochromatic wave source moves to the right at speed vs = 0.5 v, where v is the wavefront propagation speed in the medium. How do the crest spacing λ+ and observed frequency f+ change for a stationary observer in front of the source?
Selected answer Option B
During one emission period T0 = 1/f0, a wavefront travels v T0 = λ0 while the source advances by vs T0 in the same direction. Thus the spacing between successive wavefronts ahead of the source shrinks to λ+ = (v - vs)T0 = λ0(1 - M), raising the observed frequency to f+ = (f0)/(1 - M) (at M = 0.5, f+ = 2f0).

Core Curriculum · Grade 9-11
Mechanism

The Doppler effect (Christian Doppler, 1842) is the shift in observed frequency ν and wavelength λ produced when a wave source moves relative to the propagation medium. The 3D wavefront geometry makes the mechanism transparent: each successive spherical wave crest is emitted from a newly displaced source position separated by vs T0.

Equations and conservation laws

Ahead of a source moving at speed vs < cs, wave crests crowd together so the wavelength shortens to λfront = (cs - vs)T0 = λ0 (1 - (vs)/(cs)) and the observed frequency rises to **νfront = (ν0)/(1 - vs/cs)**. Behind the source, wavefronts stretch out to λback = λ0 (1 + (vs)/(cs)) and the frequency drops to **νback = (ν0)/(1 + vs/cs)**.

Measurements and applications

The ratio M = vs / cs is the Mach number. As M → 1, forward wavefronts pile up constructively into a shock front (sonic boom and Mach cone with half-angle sin μ = 1/M). Doppler shifts are foundational to Doppler ultrasound imaging, weather radar, and cosmological redshift measurements.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Source Mach ratio M = vs / v: 0 … 1.25, standard value 0.55
  • Wavefront speed v: 2.2 … 5 m/s, standard value 3.6 m/s
  • Source frequency f₀: 1 … 3.5 Hz, standard value 2 Hz
  • Wavefront crest intensity I₀: 0.5 … 1.5 u., standard value 1 u.
Physical model and application
When an acoustic source moves relative to a stationary observer, wave crests ahead of the source are compressed into a shorter wavelength (higher perceived frequency), while wave crests behind the source are stretched apart (lower frequency). This Doppler shift underpins radar speed enforcement and medical ultrasound.
