Faraday's Law & Lenz's Rule in 3D

The induced electromotive force in a closed circuit is equal and opposite in sign to the time rate of change of magnetic flux through the surface bounded by the circuit.

Question
A bar magnet is rapidly pushed North-pole first (N) into a closed copper solenoid. What determines the magnitude of the induced current in the coil, and how is the coil's induced magnetic field Bind directed?
Selected answer Option B
By Faraday's law, an induced current arises only when the magnetic flux ΦB through the coil turns changes over time (dΦB/dt ≠ 0). Higher magnet oscillation frequency ω and turn count N produce a larger induced EMF ℰi. The minus sign expresses Lenz's rule: the induced current generates its own magnetic field Bind that repels the approaching magnet and attracts the receding magnet.

Core Curriculum · Grade 9-11
Mechanism

Discovered by Michael Faraday in 1831, electromagnetic induction generates an electromotive force (induced EMF ℰi) around a closed conducting loop whenever the magnetic flux Φ = ∫ B· dS threading the loop changes with time.

Equations and conservation laws

By Faraday's Law for an N-turn coil, ℰi = -N(dΦ)/(dt), driving an induced current Ii = (ℰi)/(R) through resistance R. The minus sign encodes Lenz's Law: the induced current always circulates in a direction whose own magnetic field Bind opposes the flux change ΔΦ that produced it.

Measurements and applications

Thus, plunging a magnet toward the coil ((dΦ)/(dt) > 0) induces a repulsive pole that brakes the magnet; pulling it away ((dΦ)/(dt) < 0) induces an attractive pole; and holding the magnet stationary inside ((dΦ)/(dt) = 0) yields zero current. Mechanical work done against Lenz's braking force converts directly into Joule electrical power P = Ii2 R.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Coil turn count N: 4 … 20, standard value 12
  • Magnet field B₀: 0.6 … 2.5 T, standard value 1.6 T
  • Magnet oscillation freq. ω: 0.8 … 4 rad/s, standard value 2.2 rad/s
  • Circuit resistance R: 1 … 5 Ω, standard value 2 Ω
Physical model and application
Power-plant alternators, bicycle dynamos, and inductive chargers operate by Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction: moving a magnet relative to a coil varies the magnetic flux and induces an electromotive force, whereas a stationary magnet produces zero current.
