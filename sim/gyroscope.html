Moment of Inertia & Rolling Kinetic Energy

In rolling without slipping, potential energy splits into translational and rotational kinetic energy: the body with smaller dimensionless inertia β wins.

Advanced Level · AP / University
Mechanism

When an axisymmetric rigid body of mass m and radius R rolls without slipping (v = ω R) down an incline, König’s theorem states that its total kinetic energy is the sum of translational kinetic energy Eₜᵣₐₙₛ = (1)/(2)mv² and rotational kinetic energy Eᵣₒₜ = (1)/(2)Iω².

Equations and conservation laws

Expressing the moment of inertia via the dimensionless shape factor β = (I)/(mR²) (β = 0.4 for a solid sphere, β = 0.5 for a solid cylinder/disk, β = 1.0 for a thin hoop) gives E_k = (1)/(2)mv²(1 + β). Conservation of energy mgh = (1)/(2)mv²(1 + β) yields the linear downhill acceleration a = (g sin α)/(1 + β).

Measurements and applications

Remarkably, the rolling acceleration a is completely independent of total mass m and radius R, depending only on the mass distribution factor β! Because a thin hoop (β = 1.0) diverts 50% of its energy into rotation whereas a solid disk (β = 0.5) diverts only 33.3%, the solid disk or sphere always beats the hoop to the bottom.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Inertia factor β = I/(mR²): 0.2 … 1, standard value 0.5
  • Incline angle α: 14 … 40 °, standard value 26 °
  • Rolling body radius R: 0.45 … 0.9 m, standard value 0.65 m
  • Body mass m: 1 … 4 kg, standard value 2 kg
Physical model and application
When a solid sphere and a thin-walled hoop roll without slipping down the same incline, the solid sphere achieves a higher linear acceleration regardless of total mass because its moment of inertia (I = (2)/(5)mR²) is smaller than that of the hoop (I = mR²), where mass is concentrated at the rim.

Question
A solid disk (β = 0.5) and a thin-walled hoop (β = 1.0) of equal mass and radius are released simultaneously from rest at the top of an incline and roll without slipping. Which body reaches the bottom first?
Selected answer Option B
By conservation of energy mgh = (1)/(2)mv² + (1)/(2)Iω² = (1)/(2)mv²(1 + β), where β = I/(mR²). The linear acceleration down the slope is a = (g sin α)/(1 + β). Since a solid cylinder has β = 0.5 (a = (2)/(3)g sin α) while a thin hoop has β = 1.0 (a = (1)/(2)g sin α), the solid disk always beats the hoop regardless of mass or radius!
