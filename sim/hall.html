The Hall Effect: Transverse Potential Difference & Carrier Sign Determination

Balancing the magnetic Lorentz force with the transverse electrostatic field of accumulated edge charges reveals both the concentration and sign of charge carriers.

Question
Why does measuring the polarity of the Hall voltage UH between the side faces of a conductor in a magnetic field allow us to distinguish an n-type semiconductor (electrons -e) from a p-type semiconductor (holes +e) at the exact same conventional current direction I?
Selected answer Option B
For a given conventional current I, positively charged holes (q = +e) drift along I (vd ↑↑ I), whereas negatively charged electrons (q = -e) drift opposite to I (vd ↑↓ I). Since both the charge sign q and drift velocity vd reverse simultaneously, the product qvd has the same direction in both cases! Consequently, the Lorentz force FL = q[vd×B] pushes both electrons and holes toward the exact same side face of the wafer, creating opposite signs of the transverse Hall voltage UH = RH (IB)/(d) (RH = (1)/(qn)).

Mechanism

Edwin Hall discovered this effect in 1879. When a conducting or semiconducting slab of thickness d and width w carries current I = |q|n vd w d across a perpendicular magnetic field B, drifting carriers q experience a transverse Lorentz force FB = q[vd × B] that pushes them to one side edge.

Equations and conservation laws

Charge accumulation on the side faces builds up a transverse electrostatic Hall field EH until electric force balances magnetic deflection (|q|EH = |q|vd B ⇒ EH = vd B). The resulting steady-state Hall voltage across width w is **UH = EH w = vd B w = (I B)/(n |q| d) = RH (I B)/(d)**, where RH = (1)/(nq) is the Hall coefficient.

Measurements and applications

The Hall effect is a cornerstone of solid-state physics and sensor engineering: 1) measuring UH directly reveals the **carrier concentration n**; 2) the polarity of UH unambiguously identifies the sign of the majority charge carriers (n-type electrons vs. p-type holes), since both are deflected to the same side of the slab!

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Longitudinal current I: 1 … 6 A, standard value 3 A
  • Magnetic induction B: 0.5 … 3 T, standard value 1.5 T
  • Carrier concentration n: 0.8 … 5 ×10²² m⁻³, standard value 2 ×10²² m⁻³
  • Wafer thickness d: 0.5 … 2.5 mm, standard value 1 mm
  • Conductivity type (−1 n-type, +1 p-type): -1 … 1, standard value -1
Physical model and application
Semiconductor Hall sensors are widely used in brushless motors, automotive ignition timing, and magnetometers: when a perpendicular magnetic field penetrates a current-carrying strip, the Lorentz force deflects charge carriers toward one lateral edge, generating a measurable transverse Hall voltage.
