Motion of a Body on an Inclined Plane

Resolving gravity along mutually perpendicular axes determines the equilibrium condition and acceleration of a body on a slope.

Question
A block rests on an inclined plane at angle α = 30° with sliding friction coefficient μ = 0.25. Under what condition will the block start sliding down on its own?
Selected answer Option B
In the inequality mg sin α > μ mg cos α, the mass m cancels out! Thus, the condition for sliding depends solely on the incline angle and friction coefficient: tan α > μ (the incline angle must exceed the angle of repose φ = arctan μ).
Formula
a = g( sin α - μ cos α), Ffric = μ mg cos α, tan α > μ

Mechanism

A block of mass m resting on an inclined plane at angle α is acted upon by three forces: gravity mg pointing vertically downward, the normal reaction N perpendicular to the ramp, and friction Ffr directed up the slope opposing motion. Resolving mg into components parallel (Ox) and perpendicular (Oy) to the incline reduces the problem to 1D dynamics.

Equations and conservation laws

Force balance normal to the ramp yields N = mg cos α. By Amontons-Coulomb's law, the maximum static friction is Ffr,max = μ N = μ mg cos α. Motion begins only when the downhill gravitational component exceeds this threshold (tan α > μ), producing a uniform acceleration a = g( sin α - μ cos α) that is strictly independent of the mass m.

Measurements and applications

The threshold angle αc = arctan (μ) is known as the angle of repose, widely used in tribology and geotechnical engineering to measure μ directly. Watch the energy balance chart: initial potential energy Ep = mgh partitions into translational kinetic energy Ek = (1)/(2)mv2 and frictional heat Q = μ mg cos α · s.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Incline angle α: 5 … 55 °, standard value 30 °
  • Friction coeff. μ: 0 … 0.85, standard value 0.25
  • Block mass m: 0.5 … 6 kg, standard value 2.5 kg
  • Gravity g: 1.6 … 15 m/s², standard value 9.81 m/s²
Physical model and application
When a block slides down an inclined plane, gravity resolves into a longitudinal component mg sin α driving motion downhill and a normal component mg cos α pressing the body against the surface. Increasing the inclination angle α increases the downhill driving force while reducing normal pressure.
