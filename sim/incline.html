Motion of a Body on an Inclined Plane

Resolving gravity along mutually perpendicular axes determines the equilibrium condition and acceleration of a body on a slope.

Grade 7–9
Mechanism

A block of mass m resting on an inclined plane at angle α is acted upon by three forces: gravity mg pointing vertically downward, the normal reaction N perpendicular to the ramp, and friction F_fr directed up the slope opposing motion. Resolving mg into components parallel (Ox) and perpendicular (Oy) to the incline reduces the problem to 1D dynamics.

Equations and conservation laws

Force balance normal to the ramp yields N = mg cos α. By Amontons–Coulomb’s law, the maximum static friction is F_fr,max = μ N = μ mg cos α. Motion begins only when the downhill gravitational component exceeds this threshold (tan α > μ), producing a uniform acceleration a = g(sin α - μcos α) that is strictly independent of the mass m.

Measurements and applications

The threshold angle α_c = arctan(μ) is known as the angle of repose, widely used in tribology and geotechnical engineering to measure μ directly. Watch the energy balance chart: initial potential energy E_p = mgh partitions into translational kinetic energy E_k = (1)/(2)mv² and frictional heat Q = μ mg cos α · s.

Theoretical derivation and analysis

In the inequality mg sin α > μ mg cos α, the mass m cancels out! Thus, the condition for sliding depends solely on the incline angle and friction coefficient: tan α > μ (the incline angle must exceed the angle of repose φ = arctanμ).

Methodological calculation guide

Compare the downhill gravity component mg sin α against the maximum friction force μ mg cos α holding the block back.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Incline angle α: 5 … 55 °, standard value 30 °
  • Friction coeff. μ: 0 … 0.85, standard value 0.25
  • Block mass m: 0.5 … 6 kg, standard value 2.5 kg
  • Gravity g: 1.6 … 15 m/s², standard value 9.81 m/s²
Everyday example
Picture sledding down a snowy hill or sliding a book down a tilted desk. The steeper the slope (larger angle α), the harder gravity pulls you downhill and the less it presses you into the surface!
ma = mg + N + F_fric ; a = g(sin α - μcos α), N = mg cos α
