Magnus Effect & Lift Force on a Rotating Cylinder

Rotation entrains the boundary layer, creating a velocity asymmetry and a transverse Bernoulli pressure gradient.

Grade 10
Mechanism

The Magnus effect (Heinrich Gustav Magnus, 1852) is the generation of a transverse aerodynamic lift force F_M on a spinning cylinder or sphere moving through a fluid of density ρ at freestream velocity v_∞.

Equations and conservation laws

Through boundary-layer entrainment, a cylinder of radius R spinning at angular speed ω establishes a net velocity circulation Gamma = ∮ v· dl = 2π R² ω. On the side where surface rotation moves with the freestream, flow speeds add (v_top = v_∞ + ω R); on the opposite side, flow is retarded (v_bot = v_∞ - ω R). By Bernoulli’s theorem p + (1)/(2)ρ v² = const, higher velocity produces lower static pressure (p_top < p_bot)!

Measurements and applications

Integrating pressure around a cylinder of span L yields the Kutta–Joukowski lift theorem: F_M = ρ v_∞ Gamma L = 2π ρ R² L ω v_∞, directed along ω × v_∞. This mechanism explains curved trajectories of spinning soccer, tennis, and baseballs as well as Flettner rotor ships.

Theoretical derivation and analysis

Where the spinning cylinder surface moves in the same direction as the oncoming stream, the resultant flow speed is higher (v + ω R), and by Bernoulli's equation P + (1)/(2)ρ v² = const the static pressure is lower. This pressure difference produces the transverse Kutta–Joukowski–Magnus lift F_M = ρ v_∞ Gamma L.

Methodological calculation guide

On which side of the spinning sphere is air speed higher and pressure lower, where the surface spins with the airflow or against it?

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Free-stream flow velocity v_∞: 3 … 15 m/s, standard value 8 m/s
  • Cylinder angular velocity ω: -40 … 40 rad/s, standard value 25 rad/s
  • Rotor radius R: 0.8 … 1.8 dm, standard value 1.2 dm
  • Fluid density ρ: 0.6 … 2.4 kg/m³, standard value 1.25 kg/m³
Everyday example
Ever seen a soccer player bend a free kick around a defensive wall or a baseball pitcher throw a curveball? A spinning ball drags air faster on one side than the other; by Bernoulli’s principle, the pressure difference pushes the ball sideways!
