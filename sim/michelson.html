Michelson Interferometer & Phase Shift of Beams

Displacing a mirror by merely half a wavelength of light reverses the central interference fringe from bright to dark.

Question
By what distance Δ d must the movable mirror M1 of a Michelson interferometer be translated for the interference pattern on the screen to shift by exactly one full fringe (Δ m = 1)?
Selected answer Option B
In the arm with movable mirror M1, the light beam travels from the beam splitter to the mirror and back again. Moving the mirror by Δ d therefore changes the round-trip optical path difference by Δ = 2Δ d. Shifting by one full fringe requires 2Δ d = λ, or Δ d = λ/2.

Mechanism

Invented by Albert A. Michelson in 1881, the Michelson interferometer splits a coherent monochromatic light beam of wavelength λ at a 45° beam splitter (BS) into two orthogonal arms of lengths L1 and L2. After reflecting off mirrors M1 (micrometer-adjustable) and M2 (fixed), the beams recombine at BS and interfere on the detector screen.

Equations and conservation laws

Because light traverses arm 1 twice (out and back), translating mirror M1 by distance Δ d changes the round-trip optical path length by **Δ = 2Δ d**, introducing a phase shift Δφ = (4π Δ d)/(λ). Central fringe intensity varies as I(Δ d) = Imax cos 2 ((2π Δ d)/(λ)).

Measurements and applications

Consequently, moving mirror M1 by just a quarter-wavelength (Δ d = λ/4) flips a bright maximum into a dark minimum, and every Δ d = λ/2 shifts the pattern by one full fringe (N = (2Δ d)/(λ)). This design powered the historic 1887 Michelson-Morley experiment and forms the optical core of the LIGO gravitational-wave observatories.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Laser wavelength λ: 420 … 680 nm, standard value 550 nm
  • Micrometer mirror displacement Δd: 0 … 700 nm, standard value 0 nm
  • Laser beam power P₀: 2 … 10 mW, standard value 5 mW
Physical model and application
The Michelson interferometer is a precision optical instrument capable of resolving sub-wavelength displacements. A coherent laser beam is split into two orthogonal arms and recombined after mirror reflection: displacing the movable mirror by half a wavelength (λ/2) shifts the interference pattern by one full fringe.
