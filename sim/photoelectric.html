External Photoelectric Effect: Einstein's Equation & Stopping Potential

Light is absorbed in discrete quanta hν: the maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons depends solely on light frequency and cathode work function.

Question
According to Einstein's photoelectric equation hν = Aw + Ek,max, how does doubling the light intensity I (at constant wavelength λ < λmax) affect the maximum kinetic energy Ek,max of emitted photoelectrons and saturation photocurrent Isat?
Selected answer Option B
Each individual photon carries energy Eγ = hν = (hc)/(λ) ≈ (1240 eV·nm)/(λ). An electron absorbs a single photon and spends the work function Aw to escape the metal, leaving maximum kinetic energy Ek,max = eUstop = (hc)/(λ) - Aw. Increasing light intensity I increases the number of photons per second (increasing saturation current Isat), but does not change the energy of each photon hν!

Core Curriculum · Grade 11
Mechanism

The external photoelectric effect, emission of electrons from a metal surface illuminated by light, defied classical wave electrodynamics, which could not explain why maximum photoelectron kinetic energy is independent of light intensity and why emission ceases abruptly above a threshold wavelength λmax = hc / W.

Equations and conservation laws

In 1905, Albert Einstein resolved the paradox by proposing that light consists of discrete energy quanta (photons) of energy Eγ = hν = (hc)/(λ), where h = 6.626× 10-34 J·s is Planck's constant. A single electron absorbs one photon, expends the metal's work function W to escape the surface barrier, and retains maximum kinetic energy given by Einstein's photoelectric equation: hν = W + Ek,max.

Measurements and applications

Applying a reverse bias halts the photocurrent at the stopping potential Us, where e Us = Ek,max = (hc)/(λ) - W. Increasing optical intensity (for λ < λmax) increases the photon flux and saturation photocurrent Isat proportionally without altering Ek,max.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Incident wavelength λ: 250 … 650 nm, standard value 380 nm
  • Cathode work function Aw (Cs=1.9, K=2.2, Zn=4.3): 1.8 … 4.5 eV, standard value 2.2 eV
  • Photon flux intensity Φ: 0.5 … 3 W/m², standard value 1.5 W/m²
  • Retarding voltage Uret: 0 … 2.5 V, standard value 0.4 V
Physical model and application
Photodiodes, solar cells, and optical sensors operate via the photoelectric effect. Electromagnetic radiation is absorbed in discrete quanta of energy E = hν: photons with energy below the metal's work function W cannot eject electrons regardless of beam intensity, whereas photons with ν > W/h trigger immediate photoelectron emission.
