Gyroscopic Precession of a Heavy Symmetric Top

The axis of a rapidly spinning gyroscope moves not in the direction of the applied force, but in the direction of its torque vector.

Question
How does the angular speed of gyroscopic precession Ωprec = (mgd)/(Iω) change if the flywheel is spun up to twice its initial angular velocity ω?
Selected answer Option B
From the torque relation dL = L sin θ Ωprec dt = mgd sin θ dt, we obtain Ωprec = (mgd)/(Iω). Thus, the larger the intrinsic spin angular momentum L = Iω, the slower the rotor axis precesses.

Mechanism

A fast spinning top defies intuition. Tilted at angle θ, it does not fall; instead, its axis sweeps a steady horizontal circle because external torque equals the vector rate of change of angular momentum, (dL)/(dt) = τ.

Equations and conservation laws

A rotor with moment of inertia I spinning rapidly at ωs carries a large spin angular momentum L = Iωs directed along its symmetry axis. Gravity mg acting at distance l from the pivot O exerts a torque τ = [r × mg] of magnitude τ = mgl sin θ that points **horizontally and perpendicular to L**.

Measurements and applications

Because the infinitesimal increment dL = τ dt is orthogonal to L, it rotates the direction of L around the vertical axis without changing its tilt angle θ. The angular velocity of this gyroscopic precession is **Ωp = (τ)/(L sin θ) = (mgl)/(Iωs)**: spinning the rotor faster (ωs ↑) makes the precession slower and more stable!

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Rotor spin angular velocity ω: 15 … 70 rad/s, standard value 35 rad/s
  • Gyroscope flywheel mass m: 1 … 3.5 kg, standard value 2 kg
  • Center-of-mass lever arm d: 0.6 … 2 m, standard value 1.2 m
  • Nutation (axis tilt) angle θ: 15 … 55 °, standard value 32 °
Physical model and application
A rapidly spinning gyrocompass rotor or flywheel resists tipping under gravity due to its large spin angular momentum L = Iω: instead of falling downward under gravitational torque M, the spin axis sweeps steadily around a horizontal circle in gyroscopic precession.
