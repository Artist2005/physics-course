Rutherford's Gold Foil Experiment & Planetary Model of the Atom

The backscattering of fast alpha particles from a thin gold foil revealed that the entire positive charge and mass of the atom are concentrated in a tiny nucleus.

Question
How does the scattering angle θ of an α-particle depend on its impact parameter b (the perpendicular distance from the initial velocity line to the center of the target nucleus +Ze)?
Selected answer Option B
An α-particle (qα = +2e) moving in the repulsive Coulomb field of a heavy nucleus (Q = +Ze) follows a hyperbola governed by Rutherford's relation cot (θ/2) = (2b)/(rmin), where rmin = (2Ze2)/(4πε0 Eα) is the closest approach distance in a head-on collision (b = 0). When b is large, the particle passes far from the nucleus (θ ≈ 0°); when b 90°).

Core Curriculum · Grade 9-11
Mechanism

Before 1911, J. J. Thomson's "plum-pudding" model pictured positive charge spread diffusely across the entire atomic volume (Ratom ∼ 10-10 m), predicting that fast α-particles (qα = +2e) would traverse thin gold foil with negligible deflection (θ 90°!

Equations and conservation laws

Rutherford concluded that all positive charge +Ze and 99.97% of atomic mass reside in a tiny central atomic nucleus (Rnuc ∼ 10-15 m). Repelled by the inverse-square Coulomb force F(r) = (1)/(4πε0)(2Ze2)/(r2), an α-particle of kinetic energy Eα and impact parameter b follows a hyperbolic trajectory with scattering angle given by Rutherford's formula: cot ((θ)/(2)) = (4πε0 Eα b)/(Z e2) = (2b)/(dmin).

Measurements and applications

In a head-on collision (b → 0, θ → 180°), kinetic energy converts entirely into electrostatic potential energy at the distance of closest approach dmin = (2Ze2)/(4πε0 Eα), establishing the planetary nuclear model of the atom.

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Impact parameter b: 3 … 60 fm, standard value 18 fm
  • α-particle kinetic energy Eα: 3 … 9 MeV, standard value 5.5 MeV
  • Target nuclear charge Z: 20 … 92 e, standard value 79 e
  • Beam spread width: 0.5 … 1.5 ×, standard value 1 ×
Physical model and application
In Ernest Rutherford's gold-foil experiment, the vast majority of incident α-particles passed through thin gold foil with minimal deflection, while a tiny fraction scattered through angles exceeding 90°, demonstrating that an atom's positive charge and mass are concentrated in a compact, dense nucleus.
