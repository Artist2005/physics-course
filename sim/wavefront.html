Light Wavefront & Huygens–Fresnel Principle

Every point of a medium reached by a light wavefront becomes a source of secondary hemispherical wavelets, whose envelope forms the new wavefront at the next instant.

Question
A plane light wavefront passes from air (n1 = 1.0) into optically denser glass (n2 = 1.5) at an angle of incidence α > 0. Why does the plane of the light wavefront bend toward the normal (γ < α)?
Selected answer Option B
According to Huygens' principle, as an oblique light wavefront reaches the interface, its left edge enters the glass first and emits secondary hemispherical wavelets that travel at a lower phase velocity v2 = c/n2. While the right edge travels v1 Δ t in air, the left edge travels only v2 Δ t in glass, rotating the wavefront envelope so that sin α / sin γ = v1 / v2 = n2 / n1.

Mechanism

According to the Huygens-Fresnel principle, every point on an advancing light wavefront acts as a point source of secondary spherical wavelets. The wavefront surface at a later instant t + Δ t is formed by the forward tangent envelope of all these secondary wavelets.

Equations and conservation laws

When an oblique plane wavefront strikes the interface between media of refractive indices n1 and n2 > n1 at angle α, its phase velocity drops from v1 = c/n1 to v2 = c/n2. While the outer edge of the wavefront travels distance BC = v1 Δ t = AB sin α in medium 1, the edge that entered medium 2 expands only to radius AD = v2 Δ t = AB sin γ. Taking the ratio immediately proves Snell's Law of Refraction: ( sin α)/( sin γ) = (v1)/(v2) = (n2)/(n1).

Measurements and applications

Observe in 3D how entering optically denser glass (n2 > n1) compresses the spacing between wavefront crests (λ2 = λ0 / n2) while keeping frequency ν constant, rotating the wavefront normal toward the interface normal (γ < α).

Experimental parameters and SI units
  • Wavefront incidence angle α: 10 … 75 °, standard value 45 °
  • Medium refractive index n₂: 1.15 … 2.4, standard value 1.55
  • Vacuum wavelength λ₀: 400 … 700 nm, standard value 540 nm
  • Huygens wavelet sources N: 3 … 7 sources, standard value 5 sources
Physical model and application
When a two-wheeled axle rolls obliquely from smooth pavement onto soft sand, the wheel that crosses the boundary first slows down while the second wheel continues briefly at speed v1, pivoting the axle toward the normal. By the same geometric mechanism, an oblique planar wavefront bends toward the normal upon entering an optically denser medium (n2 > n1).
